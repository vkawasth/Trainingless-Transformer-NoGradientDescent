\title{\textbf{Pooling idempotents and the Beck defects of the absolute tower}\\[.3em]
\large A corrected form of the shadow decomposition}
\author{Vinay K. Awasthi\\ Department of Applied Mathematics and Statistics, Johns Hopkins University}
\date{October 13, 2026}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The two candidate mixed distributive laws of the absolute three-level tower \cite{Tower} fail some Beck axioms. We show that every
defect of the shift law is governed by one natural transformation, the pooling idempotent $e=\eta_{\D}\circ\mu\colon\D^2\Rightarrow\D^2$,
which sends a law on laws to the point mass at its barycenter. At level $k$ the unit axiom holds up to the nested pooling
$P_k=\eta\circ\D^{k-1}(\mu)$: the left side is $P_k$ applied to the right side, and the right side is reached through $k$ stages, one
pooling per depth. The multiplication axiom fails by the same $P_k$, applied inside a mixture instead of outside it. Because $e$ is not
affine, the two defects point in opposite directions, which is the structural reason behind the tower paper's result that no partial
order orients both. Every statement is a one-line consequence of the monad laws and is verified in exact arithmetic, including on a
three-point base. This note replaces an earlier draft, the ``shadow decomposition'', whose 3-cells were nearest-atom quantisers; we
record what that draft got right, what it got wrong, and why the corrected objects are 2-cells of $\mathbf{Cat}$ with no 3-cells needed.
\end{abstract}

\section{What this note replaces}
An earlier draft proposed a ``shadow decomposition'' of the shift law's Beck defects into crossover maps $\chi_k$, coherence 2-cells
$\alpha_k$ and 3-cells $\beta_k$, said to be conditional expectations onto the $\sigma$-algebras generated by a context, and asserted the
coherence axioms of a tricategory. Its scripts were audited for this note (\cref{sec:audit}). Three findings decided the rewrite.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item The maps $\beta_k$ send each support point to its nearest atom in total variation, with ties broken by the order of a list. That is a
  quantiser, not a conditional expectation: it does not preserve barycenters, it depends on the listing of the atoms, and on a three-point
  base it fails to commute with pushforward along a Markov kernel in about half of all random cases.
\item It does not decompose the defect: in the draft's own two-atom example, $\beta_1$ does not send the right side of (U) to its left
  side.
\item The ``coherence'' checks in the scripts were recorded as true without being computed, and the first script's $\beta_1$ returns the
  empty distribution on the standard input, so its checks passed vacuously.
\end{enumerate}
The draft's instinct was right: in the single-atom case $\beta_1$ does undo the unit defect, by pooling. The corrected object is the
barycentric pooling map, and it decomposes every defect exactly.

\section{Setup}
We use the notation of \cite{Tower}. $\D$ is the finite distribution monad with unit $\eta$ (point mass) and multiplication $\mu$
(barycenter). The absolute tower is the context comonad $\C_3X=X\times\E_3X$ with $\E_3X=\D X\times\D^2X\times\D^3X\times\Sh X$ and
points $(x,c)$, $c=(\rho,\Pi,\Omega,\Sigma)$ \cite[Thm.~6.1, Cor.~6.4]{Tower}. The shift law is
\[
\ls_X(M)=\Big(\sum_ia_i\delta_{x_i},\ \sum_ia_i\delta_{\rho_i},\ \sum_ia_i\delta_{\Pi_i},\ \sum_ia_i\delta_{\Omega_i},\ \sum_ia_i(K^{[1]})_*\Sigma_i\Big)
\]
for $M=\sum_ia_i\delta_{(x_i,c_i)}$, with $K^{[1]}(\theta,\rho)=(\theta,\delta_\rho)$; it is natural, satisfies (Co), and fails (U), (M) and
(Cm) \cite[Thm.~8.3]{Tower}. The averaging law $\lb$ satisfies (U), (Co), (M) and fails only (Cm) \cite[Thms.~8.6, 8.7]{Tower}. The Beck
axioms are read left to right as in \cite[Def.~2.1]{Tower}. Note that $\ls$ is affine: each component is a mixture over the samples, so
$\ls\circ\mu_{\C_3}=\beta\circ\D(\ls)$, where $\beta$ is mixing in $\C_3\D X$ (componentwise barycenter, \cite[\S8]{Tower}).

\section{The pooling idempotent}
\begin{definition}[Pooling]\label{def:e}
$e_Y=\eta_{\D Y}\circ\mu_Y\colon\D^2Y\to\D^2Y$ sends $\nu=\sum_j\nu_j\delta_{p_j}$ to $\delta_{\sum_j\nu_jp_j}$. For $k\ge1$ the \emph{nested pooling}
on $\D^{k+1}Y$ is $P_k=e\circ\D(e)\circ\cdots\circ\D^{k-1}(e)$, pooling the innermost depth first. On the context component of $\C_3\D X$ let
$P$ act by $P_k$ on the level-$k$ component and by $(\theta,\nu)\mapsto(\theta,e(\nu))$ on each hypothesis of the shadow.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:e}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item $e$ is a natural transformation $\D^2\Rightarrow\D^2$, split by $\mu$ and $\eta_\D$: $e=\eta_\D\circ\mu$ and $\mu\circ\eta_\D=\id$. In
  particular $e$ is idempotent and preserves the barycenter, $\mu\circ e=\mu$.
\item $e$ commutes with $\D(k^\#)$ for every Markov kernel $k$, since $k^\#$ is affine.
\item $e$ is not affine: $e(\tfrac12\delta_{\delta_0}+\tfrac12\delta_{\delta_1})=\delta_{(\frac12,\frac12)}\ne\tfrac12\delta_{\delta_0}+\tfrac12\delta_{\delta_1}$.
\item $P_k=\eta\circ\D^{k-1}(\mu)$. For $k\ge2$, $P_k$ is not idempotent in general.
\item $e\circ\D(\eta)=\eta_\D$, and more generally $P_k\circ\D^k(\eta)=\eta_{\D^k}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) Naturality of $\eta$ and $\mu$; $\mu\circ\eta_\D=\id$ is a monad law, so $e\circ e=\eta_\D(\mu\eta_\D)\mu=e$ and $\mu e=\mu$. (ii)
$\mu\circ\D(k^\#)=k^\#\circ\mu$ because $k^\#$ is affine, and $\D(k^\#)$ maps point masses to point masses. (iii) Direct. (iv) By
induction, $P_k=e\circ\D(P_{k-1})=\eta\mu\circ\D(\eta)\circ\D^{k-1}(\mu)=\eta\circ\D^{k-1}(\mu)$, using $\mu\circ\D(\eta)=\id$. Since $\D(f)\circ\eta=\eta\circ f$ and $\D^{k-1}(\mu)=\D(\D^{k-2}(\mu))$,
$P_kP_k(w)=\eta\big(\eta(\D^{k-2}(\mu)\,\D^{k-1}(\mu)\,w)\big)$, which equals $P_k(w)=\eta(\D^{k-1}(\mu)w)$ only when $\D^{k-1}(\mu)w$ is
already a point mass. (v) $\D^{k-1}(\mu)\circ\D^k(\eta)=\D^{k-1}(\mu\circ\D\eta)=\id$, so $P_k\circ\D^k(\eta)=\eta$.
\end{proof}

\section{The shift law's defects}
\begin{theorem}[The unit defect]\label{thm:unit}
For every point $(x,c)$,
\[
\ls\circ\eta_{\C_3X}(x,c)\;=\;(\id\times P)\big(\C_3(\eta_X)(x,c)\big).
\]
At level $k$ the right side is $\D^k(\eta_X)(c_k)$, the left side is $\delta_{c_k}=\eta(c_k)$, and pooling one depth at a time passes
through the stages $s_j=\D^{k-j}(\eta_{\D^jX})(c_k)$, $j=0,\dots,k$, from $s_0$ (right side) to $s_k$ (left side). In the shadow each
hypothesis $(\theta,\D(\eta)\rho)$ is pooled to $(\theta,\delta_\rho)=K^{[1]}(\theta,\rho)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The components of the two sides are $\delta_{c_k}$ and $\D^k(\eta)(c_k)$ by the definitions. By \cref{lem:e}(v),
$P_k(\D^k(\eta)c_k)=\delta_{c_k}$. One pooling at depth $j$ is $\D^{k-j-1}(e)$, and
$\D^{k-j-1}(e)\circ\D^{k-j}(\eta_{\D^jX})=\D^{k-j-1}(e\circ\D(\eta_{\D^jX}))=\D^{k-j-1}(\eta_{\D^{j+1}X})$, which is $s_j\mapsto s_{j+1}$. In the
shadow, $\Sh(\eta)$ sends $(\theta,\rho)$ to $(\theta,\D(\eta)\rho)$, and $e(\D(\eta)\rho)=\delta_\rho$.
\end{proof}

So the unit defect at level $k$ is a chain of $k$ elementary poolings. Stage $s_j$ inserts the unit at depth $j$: the posterior is held as
inherited spread below depth $j$ and as a committed point above it. The draft's ``crossover maps'' compared $c_k$ with its image; the
stages are the correct intermediate objects, and its ``coherence 2-cells'' between levels are the whiskerings $\D^{k-j-1}(e)$.

\begin{theorem}[The multiplication defect]\label{thm:mult}
For $M'=\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\delta_{M_\ell}\in\D^2\C_3X$,
\[
\ls\circ\mu_{\C_3X}(M')=\beta\big(\D(\ls)M'\big),\qquad
\C_3(\mu_X)\circ\ls_{\D X}\circ\D(\ls_X)(M')=\beta\big(\D(\id\times P)\,\D(\ls)M'\big).
\]
So the left side mixes the groups' images, and the right side mixes the groups' images after pooling each one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first equation is affinity of $\ls$. For the second, the level-$k$ component of $\ls_{\D X}(\D(\ls)M')$ is $\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\delta_{z_\ell}$,
where $z_\ell$ is the level-$k$ component of $\ls(M_\ell)$, and $\C_3(\mu_X)$ applies $\D^k(\mu_X)$, which sends $\delta_{z}$ to
$\delta_{\D^{k-1}(\mu)z}=P_k(z)$ by \cref{lem:e}(iv). The shadow is the same computation one level down, and the first components
agree by (Co).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Why the orientations clash]\label{cor:clash}
If $P$ were affine, $\beta\circ\D(P)=P\circ\beta$, and the multiplication defect would read ``right $=P$(left)'', the same direction
as the unit defect, ``left $=P$(right)''. $P$ is not affine (\cref{lem:e}(iii)), and the two defects point opposite ways. The tower
paper's exchanged pair \cite[Prop.~13.17]{Tower} is the extreme case: there the right side of (M) equals $P$ applied to the right side
of (U), which equals the left side of (U), and the left side of (M) equals the right side of (U). So no partial order orients both
defects, and every order that reads pooling monotonically orients them oppositely \cite[Thm.~13.15]{Tower}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[When the defects vanish]\label{prop:identity}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item (U) holds at level $k$ iff $c_k$ is an iterated point mass of depth $k$ ($\delta_x$, $\delta_{\delta_x}$, $\delta_{\delta_{\delta_x}}$); it holds in
  the shadow iff every hypothesis in the support of $\Sigma$ has a point-mass predictive.
\item (M) holds at $M'$ iff, within every group $M_\ell$, all samples share their level-$k$ context components and these are iterated
  point masses of depth $k-1$; the shadow never obstructs (M).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) $\delta_{c_k}=\D^k(\eta)c_k$ iff $c_k$ is fixed by every inserted unit, i.e.\ an iterated point mass. (ii) By \cref{thm:mult} the two
sides agree iff $P_k(z_\ell)=z_\ell$ for every group, and $z_\ell=\sum_ia_i\delta_{c_{i,k}}$ is fixed by $\eta\circ\D^{k-1}(\mu)$ iff it is a
point mass whose atom is fixed by $\D^{k-1}(\mu)$ followed by $\eta$, which is the stated condition. In the shadow, $e$ acts on
$K^{[1]}$-images, which are already point masses.
\end{proof}

These replace the draft's identity criteria, which were stated for the quantiser.

\section{The averaging law}
The averaging law's only defect, (Cm), is pooling as well, of a different coordinate: at every level and in the shadow, its left side is
its right side with each sample's context label $e_i$ replaced by the pooled label $\bar e=\sum_ia_ie_i$, a barycenter, and this is a strict
partial order natural in $X$ \cite[Thm.~13.16]{Tower}. With one defect there is nothing to clash with, so all of $\lb$'s defects are
2-cells of one orientation. The draft's specialisation to the averaging law, a constant map onto the uniform law on the shadow's
atoms, is not label pooling and is withdrawn.

A tricategory is the wrong setting. $\mathbf{Cat}$ is a 2-category, natural transformations such as $e$ are its 2-cells, and the defects are
equations between composites of 1-cells and $e$. The axioms of a tricategory (associators, pentagonators and their modifications
\cite{GordonPowerStreet1995}) do not arise.

\section{Relation to open problems}
\paragraph{Weak distributive laws.} $e=\eta_\D\circ\mu$ is a split idempotent, the kind that weak distributive laws split
\cite{Street2009,Bohm2010}. \Cref{thm:unit} gives the shift law's unit axiom the shape of a weakened unit axiom, holding up to the
idempotent; but its multiplication axiom fails too (\cref{thm:mult}), so the shift law is not a weak law in the Street--B\"ohm sense,
as the tower paper notes \cite[Rem.~8.9]{Tower}. Whether a mixed analogue of weak laws, weakening the monad axioms of a monad--comonad
law by a split idempotent of the monad, makes the shift law a law is a precise question this note raises and does not answer.
\paragraph{Open problem 3.} For the shift law it is closed negatively for partial orders \cite[Prop.~13.17]{Tower}; \cref{cor:clash}
explains why: one idempotent, applied outside a mixture in (U) and inside it in (M). For the averaging law the open part is coherence
in an order-enriched setting \cite[Thm.~13.16]{Tower}.
\paragraph{Open problem 4.} $e$ is a non-identity 2-cell, but of $\mathbf{Cat}$, which already has them. Open problem 4 asks for a 2-category
whose 2-cells sit between the maps of the base, with a 2-monad extending $\D$; $e$ suggests what those 2-cells should compare, and nothing
here constructs the 2-monad.
\paragraph{The Giry monad.} \Cref{lem:e,thm:unit,thm:mult} use only the monad laws, the affinity of $\ls$ and of Kleisli extensions, and
the definitions. They therefore hold verbatim for any monad on a category where the shift law can be defined; for the Giry monad
\cite{Giry1982} the algebra carries over, and the measurability of the maps involved is not checked here.

\section{Exact checks and audit}\label{sec:audit}
\texttt{check\_pooling\_cells.py} uses the definitions of \texttt{check\_tower.py} verbatim and checks, in exact rational arithmetic:
\begin{enumerate}[label=P\arabic*,leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item $e$ is idempotent, preserves the barycenter, is natural for an injective and a non-injective map, and commutes with $\D(k^\#)$ for
  $200$ random kernels on a three-point base ($0$ failures);
\item $e$ is not affine;
\item \cref{thm:unit} in every component on $200$ random points, with all stages $s_j$;
\item \cref{thm:mult} on $200$ random two-level samples with up to three groups;
\item \cref{prop:identity}, in both directions, on random and planted cases;
\item the exchanged pair of \cite[Prop.~13.17]{Tower};
\item the closed form $P_k=\eta\circ\D^{k-1}(\mu)$, and non-idempotence of $P_k$ for $k\ge2$ ($198$ of $200$ cases).
\end{enumerate}
It also audits the draft. The nearest-atom map moves the barycenter of $\delta_{(9/10,1/10)}$ to $(\tfrac34,\tfrac14)$, gives different
values on $\delta_{(1/2,1/2)}$ when its two atoms are listed in opposite orders, and fails to commute with $\D(k^\#)$ in $195$ of $400$ random
cases on a three-point base. The draft's second and third scripts reproduce their stated hashes; the first gives a different hash from
the one stated, and its $\beta_1$ returns the empty distribution on $\D(\eta)\rho$. In the second and third scripts the four ``coherence''
results are written as the constant \texttt{True}, and the averaging-law test checks a constant function against its own value. All
checks of this note pass; the hashes of the script and of the draft's scripts are in \texttt{FROZEN.txt}.
