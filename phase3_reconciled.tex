\section{Phase 3, Reconciled: What the Geometry Buys and What It Does Not}
\label{sec:phase3reconciled}
Phase~3 is where the algebraic drop-in of Level~1 meets empirical descent, and
it is the one place in this pipeline every claim can be put against a control.
We report it in that spirit: three of the geometric constructs survive
measurement in a precise, bounded form; one---the strip energy $E$---does not,
and is retired here. The organising fact is a single measurable clock, the
\emph{block gradient-profile drift}
\[
  \mathrm{drift}(t) \;=\; \bigl\| \pi(t) - \pi(t-w) \bigr\|, \qquad
  \pi_a \;=\; \lVert \nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}\rVert \big/ \textstyle\sum_b \lVert \nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}\rVert,
\]
the rate at which the interaction graph of \S\ref{sec:densify} is still changing.
Everything Phase~3 does---when to stop, when to jump, what to freeze---is timed
off this one signal.

\subsection{Crystallisation is real, as a regime: why the Snapper jump works}
The crystal picture names a genuine phenomenon, provided it is stated as a
property of the \emph{settled} trajectory rather than of the whole descent. Once
$\mathrm{drift}(t)\to 0$, the parameter path becomes locally
\emph{low-order polynomial} in the step index: a degree-$\le 2$ least-squares fit
over a short window has relative residual $\sim 10^{-3}$. This is the operational
content of ``crystallisation'' --- the descent has stopped searching and is now
a deterministic, algebraically-predictable curve. It is exactly the condition
Snapper's polynomiality (\S5) requires, and where the approach is still
\emph{steep}, the extrapolation lands: the 1-shot jump to the fitted minimum
recovers the descent it skips (validation $0.066 \to 0.015$ across the alignment
approach).

The bound, stated plainly because it is where the naive reading fails. On a
\emph{flat} basin floor the same fit is just as clean ($10^{-3}$ residual) but the
jump \emph{overshoots}: extrapolating a settled-but-flat trajectory forward moves
the weights along a direction that no longer lowers the loss (measured $+0.4$
validation on every attempted jump; $0\%$ of steps culled). Crystallisation
therefore licenses the jump only on a steep, clean-basin descent; on the plateau
the correct crystallised operator is not the jump but the \emph{stop}. The
Snapper is a steep-regime move; $E$ was never what governed it.

\subsection{The geo-stop, measured: drift replaces the $E$-based criterion}
The geometry does converge before the loss --- but the operative signal is
$\mathrm{drift}(t)$, not a level set of $E$. On the real corpus, drift falls
$0.097 \to 0.013$ while the loss is still creeping down; a stop triggered on a
sustained low-drift window ends training at $\sim\!50\%$ of the step budget for a
few-percent validation cost. This is a strictly cheaper geo-stop than the
$\Phi_{cl}/\tau/r_{m2}^\sigma$ stack --- one gradient-norm profile per step, no
SVD, no Hessian-strip correlation --- and unlike $E$ it survives its control (it
moves, monotonically, and it moves before the loss does). The gluing defect
$\tau=\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{FF}}\rVert/\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{Emb}}\rVert$ is kept:
it is a real, monotone basin coordinate. The strip energy $E$ is not kept.

\subsection{What the basin is made of: the cull ledger}
The settled basin has spatial structure, and it is the interaction graph of
\S\ref{sec:densify}: an Emb/FF hub with attention peripheral early, densifying
late on corpora that recruit it. This makes Phase~3's optimiser cost
\emph{partly} reducible, and the reduction is bounded, not total.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cullable periphery.} As drift settles, the value/output
  projections' preconditioner flattens ($\mathrm{CV}(1/\!\sqrt{v})\!:\,0.57\to0.24$),
  and $W_V,W_O$ run on scalar SGD at $\sim$free validation cost --- $18\%$ of
  parameters leave the second-moment state. The cullable fraction \emph{rises}
  as drift falls: spatial and temporal cullability are the same clock.
  \item \textbf{Irreducible core.} $\mathrm{Emb}$, $W_Q$, $W_K$, and $\mathrm{FF}$
  keep dispersed, live preconditioners throughout; scalar-SGD on all of them
  fails ($+103\%$), freezing the whole core diverges ($\times 10^{2}$ loss). The
  adaptivity here is load-bearing, and no amount of settling removes it.
  \item \textbf{Unresolved (Q/K frozen shape).} Freezing $W_Q,W_K$'s second
  moment---keeping the dispersed diagonal but culling its update---costs between
  $+0.7\%$ and $+8.2\%$ across two seeds, i.e.\ at the noise floor. Whether this
  lever exists is a variance measurement we have not yet closed; it is reported
  as open, not as a win.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The strip energy $E$, retired}
$E=\sum_k\sum_i \arccos\sigma_i(U_k^\top U_{k+1})$ is a property of the
\emph{construction}, not of the trajectory. It is near-constant because it is
built from the near-degenerate singular directions of $W_K$ (measured gaps
$\sim\!0.008$), whose $\arccos$ is pinned by the Mean-Field pump; its automatic
gradient is wrong by $6.4\%$ for exactly this reason. At $P\approx 4.3\times10^6$
a random direction has $\lvert\cos(\dot\theta,\nabla E)\rvert\approx P^{-1/2}
\approx 5\times10^{-4}$, and the trajectory's value is the same order: the
instrument has no power to distinguish ``follows the level set'' from ``ambient.''
We therefore make \emph{no} dynamical claim for $E$. It was a coordinate of the
mould, not of the motion. This retirement is the price of keeping the rest
honest.

\subsection{What the ``manifold'' claim can bear}
The manifold is real in two senses and unreal in a third, and the distinction
matters. It is real as the \emph{constrained} geometry the descent lives on ---
the LayerNorm hypersphere $S^{D-1}$, the Softmax simplex --- and real as the
reproducible \emph{low-rank tangent structure} of the trajectory: five
optimisers' local tangent spaces collapse to an effective rank $3.44/5$ in two
near-orthogonal families, a result three independent instruments agree on. It is
\emph{not} real as a small function-space quotient one could optimise on: the
Jacobian $J_\theta=\partial f/\partial\theta$ is isotropic (no resolved low rank),
so there is no visible subspace to collapse the descent onto. ``The trajectory is
a low-rank constrained object'' holds; ``solve on the quotient'' does not.

\noindent
The reading that survives: Phase~3 crystallises a \emph{predictable} trajectory
on a \emph{constrained, low-rank} basin, and that predictability is what the
Snapper exploits and what the drift-stop detects. What does not survive is the
claim that any single scalar ($E$) or small quotient carries the dynamics. The
crystal is real; the mould-coordinate $E$ was mistaken for it.
