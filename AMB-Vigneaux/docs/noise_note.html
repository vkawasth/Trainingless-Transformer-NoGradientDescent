Loop Holonomy Under Noise

What happens to a loop's holonomy when the operations on its edges become lossy channels, measured at each of the three layers: operations, probability, outcomes. Every number below is produced by the package and checked by its test suite (81 tests passing).

Summary

For shift-covariant uniform noise, the loop operator's character eigenvalues are (1−ε)^ℓ · e^{2πikh/n}. Their modulus determines every Shannon functional of the loop channel, and their phase determines the holonomy h. Per-context and loop-composite information are independent of h. On the edge-context model the contextual fraction is max(0, 1 − ℓ_min·ε/2): it is certified by an LP dual and is not determined by information functionals. The labelled phase detects h for every ε < 1, including ε ≥ 2/ℓ_min, where CF = 0. The three layers respond to the same noise with different invariants, and no invariant of one layer determines another.

Setting

A connected graph Γ, a finite abelian group A (mostly Z/n), and on each edge (a,b) a doubly-stochastic channel K_ab: row x is the law of the colour at b given colour x at a. Colours are uniform at every vertex, which every doubly-stochastic channel preserves, so traversing an edge backwards uses Kᵀ, its exact Bayesian inverse. The main family is the noisy shift:

K_ab = (1−ε)·S_{g_ab} + ε·U, S_g : x ↦ x+g, U = (1/n)·𝟙𝟙ᵀ

The loop operator M_L is the product of the edge channels around a loop L. The edge-context model e_K takes the vertex colours as measurements, the edges as contexts, and (1/n)·K_ab as the law on edge (a,b). At ε = 0 it is the all-versus-nothing model e_g (an instance of the all-versus-nothing models of Abramsky et al., 2015). Contextuality is measured by the contextual fraction (Abramsky–Barbosa–Mansfield 2017) and the Čech obstruction γ (Abramsky–Mansfield–Barbosa 2012).

Results

Proved

1 · The loop operator

M_L = (1−ε)^|L| · S_h + [1 − (1−ε)^|L|] · U, h = holonomy of L λ_k = (1−ε)^|L| · e^{2πikh/n} (k ≠ 0), λ_0 = 1

Shift-covariant channels commute, and S_g U = U S_g = U. The eigenvalue labelled by a character χ does not depend on where the loop starts, because all the channels are diagonal in the same character basis. Tested for every h and ε ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.9}.

ProvedCorrected formula

2 · Layer 2 information is blind to h

Each noisy row puts mass 1−ε+ε/n on one colour and ε/n on each of the other n−1. With a uniform input:

I(c_a; c_b) = log₂n + (1−ε+ε/n)·log₂(1−ε+ε/n) + (n−1)(ε/n)·log₂(ε/n) I_loop = the same formula with ε replaced by ε_L = 1 − (1−ε)^|L|

Neither depends on g or h. More generally, any per-context Shannon functional is unchanged when an edge's output is permuted. That holds for lossy channels too: N·S_g has the row entropies of N.

3 · The labelled eigenvalue carries h; the plain spectrum does not

  • The unlabelled spectrum only sees the order of h. As a multiset it is base-point free for any channels. On Z/5, every h ≠ 0 gives the same multiset.
  • The eigenvalue labelled by a character reads h exactly for every ε < 1. For general covariant noise μ it equals Π_e μ̂_e(χ) · χ(h), so its phase is χ(h) exactly when μ̂(χ) is real and nonzero, which is the case for symmetric noise.
  • Asymmetric noise adds its own phase. With μ = (0.7, 0.2, 0, 0, 0.1) and h = 2, the readout is 2.285, offset by the noise-only phase of 0.285.
  • The same holds for any finite abelian group A = ⊕ Z/m_j. Tested on Z/2×Z/2, Z/2×Z/4 and Z/3×Z/3.
Proved

4 · The contextual fraction on one cycle

CF(e_K) = max(0, 1 − |L|·ε/2) for any h ≠ 0, any n, any finite abelian A

Lower bound (dual certificate). Weight each edge cell by its discrepancy d = y − x − g: w(0) = 0, w(−h) = 1, and w = ½ otherwise. Every global colouring has discrepancies summing to −h ≠ 0 around the loop. So it has either one nonzero discrepancy, equal to −h (weight 1), or at least two (weight ≥ 1). The certificate's value is |L|·(ε/n)·(1 + (n−2)/2) = |L|ε/2, which bounds the non-contextual fraction.

Upper bound (packing). Take colourings with one violation of discrepancy −h, and colourings with two violations (a, −h−a), each spread evenly over translations. Together they use all the off-graph mass and fit within the on-graph capacity, which becomes exactly tight at ε = 2/|L|.

For n = 2 this is the chained Bell inequality. The LP matches the formula for n ∈ {2, 3, 5}, |L| = 3–6, and every h tested. The certificate is checked exhaustively over all colourings for five (n, |L|, h) cases.

Lower bound provedEquality measured

5 · Any graph: the shortest non-flat cycle decides

CF(e_K) = max(0, 1 − ℓ_min·ε/2), ℓ_min = shortest simple cycle with nonzero holonomy
  • Proved: CF ≥ this value. Restrict the single-cycle certificate to the shortest non-flat cycle.
  • Measured: equality. It holds in 44 configurations: θ-graphs, K4, loops sharing an edge, loops sharing a vertex, disjoint non-flat loops, and ε up to 0.8.
  • The cycle basis is irrelevant. In θ(2,2,5), when the 4-cycle is flat and only the 7-cycles carry holonomy, CF follows 7, not 4.

Open: a proof of the packing on general graphs.

Does not hold

6 · Information does not determine CF

  • Same ε, different CF. Every edge has identical per-edge information, yet on a 3-cycle and a 5-cycle at ε = 0.2 the contextual fraction is 0.70 and 0.50.
  • Noise that doesn't commute with the shifts is contextual on its own. A flat system then reaches CF up to 0.24, and the phase readout of h = 2 spreads over 1.1–3.1.
  • Entropy is not a function of Fourier moduli. A homometric pair a∗b and a∗b̃ has identical |FFT| but H = 2.7714 against 2.7689 bits. "I depends only on |λ_k|" is true for the uniform-noise family only.

Open problems

  1. Prove equality in Result 5: a packing of colourings on general graphs, extending the cycle packing along a spanning tree.
  2. Find the invariant for non-commuting noise, where noise and holonomy mix and flat systems are already contextual.
  3. Estimate the labelled eigenvalue from edge readings. This connects to Problem 26: which designs make the phase identifiable, and at what sample cost?
