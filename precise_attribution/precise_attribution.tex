\begin{abstract}
A language model is trained on a corpus, extended with a second corpus, edited directly in its
weights, and tuned with reinforcement learning from human feedback. Each step changes some outputs
and leaves others alone, and new outputs appear. We ask three questions about such a history: what
explains each observed change, how precisely the data determine that explanation, and whether the
answer depends on the order of the steps. We answer them with three notions. An \emph{attribution
set} collects every decomposition of an observed output distribution into declared sources and an
unmodeled remainder; it is a polytope, and its emptiness, its range of unmodeled weight and its
certificates are computed by linear programming. \emph{Precision} is the width of the attribution
set; it is monotone along a ladder from exact sources to sources known by their supports to a
positive budget for unmodeled sources, and any slack in that budget forces imprecision.
\emph{Mechanism reach} declares what an intervention type may change: we prove that the reach set of
KL-regularized reinforcement learning with a bounded reward range is a polytope, so that attributing
a change to it is a linear program with an exact certificate, whereas a fixed reward with unknown
temperature gives a curve, and an unconstrained edit can explain anything. Finally, interventions are
compared quantitatively: comparisons compose by the triangle inequality and by a Lipschitz rule, with
exact Lipschitz constants for mixing and bounds for tilts, and a weight-level intervention may have no
Lipschitz constant on observed outputs at all. Order and loop defects measure path dependence, and
coarse evaluations certify fine differences. The three kinds of uncertainty involved (who could
have produced an output, what a mechanism can produce, and what observation reveals about an
intervention) arise from three maps that fail to be injective; we make their interaction precise
through the observable reach of an intervention and a soundness condition for mechanism classes, and
show that the parameters of a reachable tilt are identified exactly up to the slack in the reward
range. We then supply the glue between local structures. Markov kernels are the 1-cells; they
transport attribution sets and can only lose precision. Couplings are 2-cells that witness how two
1-cells differ. Their vertical composition is the gluing lemma and is strictly associative, but the
natural whiskerings are not functorial, and the strict 2-categorical structure is a graded metric
shadow. For several contexts, two compatible contexts always glue, and that gluing is vertical
composition of witnesses. For a general cover, the contextual fraction of Abramsky, Barbosa and
Mansfield equals our minimal unmodeled weight, and the best approximate global state is computed by a
linear program; for the Popescu--Rohrlich box it is exactly $1/4$ away from some context. Gluing at
the second level of the tower retains correlations that gluing flattened states loses. Finally, the
simplex of output distributions is a moment polytope whose Lagrangian fibres are what observation
forgets, and we state necessary conditions for a Fukaya-type realization. A synthetic four-stage
experiment produces a full provenance report. Every theorem is checked in exact arithmetic or with exactly verified linear
programming certificates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%=====================================================================

Consider the life of a model. Corpus $C_1$ produces model $M_1$. Adding corpus $C_2$ produces $M_2$,
which changes some outputs, leaves others unchanged, and makes outcomes probable that were not before.
An engineer then edits weights directly, producing $M_3$, and reinforcement learning from human
feedback (RLHF) produces $M_4$. For such a history we want answers to three questions.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(Q\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Attribution.} Which declared source or intervention explains each observed change, and
  what is left unexplained?
\item \emph{Precision.} How tightly do the observations determine that explanation?
\item \emph{Path dependence.} Does the result depend on the order of interventions, and does a
  sequence that should cancel actually cancel?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Three kinds of uncertainty.} Each question concerns a different map that fails to be
injective, and the paper is organized around them.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.2\textwidth}p{0.19\textwidth}p{0.27\textwidth}p{0.26\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Question & Object & Source of the uncertainty & Measured and certified by\\
\midrule
Who could have produced $p$? & attribution set $W_{\bar\mu}(p)$ (\S\ref{sec:attribution}) &
inverse image of $p$ under the mixing map from decompositions to outputs & widths
(\cref{def:precision}); dual certificate (\cref{thm:attset})\\
What can mechanism $T$ produce? & reach set $\Reach_T(p)$ (\S\ref{sec:mechanisms}) & image of the
mechanism's parameters, which may themselves not be identified & residual weight and certificate
(\cref{thm:change}); parameter widths (\cref{prop:reward-ident})\\
What can we observe about $T$? & $\Phi\circ T$ (\S\ref{sec:composition}) & quotient by $\Phi$, which
merges models with equal probe outputs & comparisons, Lipschitz constants and defects
(\cref{thm:composition})\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The second and third interact: an intervention acts on model states, so what can be seen of it from
observed outputs is set-valued even when the intervention is deterministic. The \emph{observable reach}
and the \emph{soundness} of a mechanism class (\cref{def:sound,prop:sound}) make this precise, and
they separate two claims that are easily conflated: that a change is not a tilt, which is a fact about
outputs, and that a change was not produced by RLHF, which also requires the tilt class to be sound
for the procedure actually run.

\paragraph{Architecture.} The companion paper \cite{Awasthi-Tower} develops the \emph{local} structure:
an ambient probability monad $\D$, and over each outcome base $B$ a context comonad, a mixed
distributive law, admissibility data and an obstruction tower. Its \S5 establishes identifiability and
contamination results for a single output distribution. The present paper builds the
\emph{transport} half of the glue between local structures: interventions as maps between model
states, and quantitative comparisons between interventions. \Cref{sec:glue} makes the glue
explicit: kernels as 1-cells, couplings as 2-cells with a strict graded shadow, and assembly of
several contexts into a global state, exactly or approximately. \Cref{sec:architecture} says how the
layers fit together, which laws are strict, and where symplectic geometry and Fukaya categories could
enter.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Attribution sets for declared sources known exactly or by their supports, with an unmodeled
  budget; their structure, emptiness criterion and certificates (\cref{thm:attset}).
\item Precision as width, a monotone precision ladder separating structural, informational and
  unmodeled imprecision (\cref{thm:ladder}), and the fact that budget slack forces imprecision
  (\cref{prop:slack}).
\item Mechanism classes and a change-decomposition theorem with certificates (\cref{thm:change}); the
  reach set of KL-regularized RLHF with bounded rewards is a polytope (\cref{thm:tilt-polytope}),
  while a fixed reward with unknown temperature is not convex (\cref{prop:curve}).
\item Quantitative comparison of interventions with vertical and horizontal composition rules and an
  accumulation bound (\cref{thm:composition}); exact or bounded Lipschitz constants for mixing, tilts
  and coarsening, and the absence of any observable Lipschitz constant for some weight-level
  interventions (\cref{prop:lipschitz,prop:nonfaithful}); order and loop defects; monotonicity under
  coarsening (\cref{prop:coarse}).
\item Observable reach and soundness of mechanism classes, with an enlargement that keeps the RLHF
  class sound for approximate optimization (\cref{prop:sound,cor:enlarge}); identifiability of tilt
  parameters up to the slack in the reward range (\cref{prop:reward-ident}).
\item A synthetic four-stage experiment with a complete provenance report (\cref{sec:demo}).
\item The glue: kernel 1-cells transport attribution and only lose precision
  (\cref{prop:transport}); coupling 2-cells form hom-categories under gluing, with explicit failures
  of functorial whiskering (\cref{thm:witness}); the graded shadow is a strict 2-category
  (\cref{thm:shadow}).
\item Assembly: two-context gluing is vertical composition of witnesses (\cref{prop:twoctx}); the
  contextual fraction equals the minimal unmodeled weight (\cref{thm:cf}), which also detects
  prompt-dependent attribution (\cref{ex:global-attr}); bounds for approximate gluing with the exact
  value $\tfrac14$ for the PR box (\cref{prop:epsstar}); gluing at level 2 (\cref{prop:level2}).
\item An architecture that separates what is strict from what holds up to error, and a geometric
  placement with checkable conditions for a Fukaya-type realization (\cref{sec:architecture}).
\end{enumerate}
Several ingredients are known in other fields; \cref{sec:related} states precisely which, and what is
new here.

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} We do not extract hypotheses from weights: declared sources must
come from reference models, checkpoints or ablations. All guarantees concern a finite probe set.
Attribution relative to a declared mechanism is not identification of a cause; causal statements
require running the compared paths. We treat output distributions as known; finite-sample precision
is an open problem (\cref{sec:scope}).

\section{Model states and observation}\label{sec:states}
%=====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Observable states]\label{def:obs}
Fix a finite set $X$ of \emph{probes} (prompts or evaluation contexts) and, for each $x\in X$, a
finite set $B_x$ of outcomes, which may be coarse classes of completions. The \emph{observable state
space} is $\cP=\prod_{x\in X}\D(B_x)$, with
\[
d(p,q)=\max_{x\in X}\TV(p_x,q_x),\qquad \TV(p_x,q_x)=\tfrac12\textstyle\sum_b\lvert p_x(b)-q_x(b)\rvert .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Model states and interventions]
A set $\cW$ of \emph{model states} (for instance weight vectors) carries an \emph{observation map}
$\Phi\colon\cW\to\cP$ sending a model to its output distributions on the probes. An
\emph{intervention} is a map $T\colon\cW\to\cW$; its \emph{observable effect} on $M$ is the pair
$(\Phi M,\Phi TM)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Observation is not faithful]
Different models can have the same outputs on every probe, and the same intervention can then
produce different outputs. This is why statements about interventions on weights cannot in general be
reduced to statements about observable states (\cref{prop:nonfaithful}).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[New outcomes]
A softmax output assigns positive probability to every outcome, so ``a new outcome appears'' is made
precise by a threshold $\varepsilon>0$: outcome $b$ \emph{appears at level $\varepsilon$} at probe
$x$ under $M\to M'$ if $(\Phi M)_x(b)\le\varepsilon<(\Phi M')_x(b)$.
\end{definition}

From \cref{sec:attribution} to \cref{sec:mechanisms} we fix a probe, write $B$ for its finite outcome
set and $p\in\D(B)$ for an observed output distribution. All statements apply probe by probe.

\section{Attribution sets and precision}\label{sec:attribution}
%=====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Declared sources and attribution sets]\label{def:attset}
A \emph{declared source} is either \emph{exact}, a distribution $A_i\in\D(B)$, or \emph{known by its
support}, a set $S_i\subseteq B$. An \emph{unmodeled budget} is a number $\bar\mu\in[0,1]$. A
\emph{decomposition} of $p$ is a family of nonnegative measures $s_1,\dots,s_k$ on $B$, the
\emph{contributions}, such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $s_i=\lambda_iA_i$ for some $\lambda_i\ge0$ if source $i$ is exact, and $\supp s_i\subseteq S_i$
  if source $i$ is known by its support, in which case $\lambda_i=\mass(s_i)$;
\item $\sum_is_i\le p$ pointwise, and $\mu:=1-\sum_i\lambda_i\le\bar\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{unmodeled part} is $p-\sum_is_i=\mu U$ with $U\in\D(B)$ when $\mu>0$. The \emph{attribution
set} $W_{\bar\mu}(p)$ is the set of all decompositions. The \emph{minimal unmodeled weight}
$\mu_{\min}(p)$ is the least $\mu$ over decompositions with $\bar\mu=1$.
\end{definition}

The model is Huber's contamination model \cite{Huber1964} with declared sources. A decomposition
determines $U$, so the attribution set records exactly the explanations compatible with $p$.

\begin{theorem}[Structure of attribution sets]\label{thm:attset}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $W_{\bar\mu}(p)$ is a polytope, cut out by linear constraints on the contributions.
\item $W_{\bar\mu}(p)\ne\emptyset$ if and only if $\mu_{\min}(p)\le\bar\mu$.
\item If $W_{\bar\mu}(p)\ne\emptyset$, the unmodeled weights it contains form the interval
  $[\mu_{\min}(p),\bar\mu]$.
\item (Certificate) Suppose $y\in\mathbb{R}^B_{\ge0}$ satisfies $\langle y,a\rangle\ge1$ for every
  admissible version $a$ of every source, that is $\langle y,A_i\rangle\ge1$ for exact sources and
  $y_b\ge1$ for $b\in S_i$ for support-known sources. Then every decomposition has
  $\mu\ge1-\langle y,p\rangle$, and the best such bound equals $\mu_{\min}(p)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Constraints (a) and (b) are linear in the variables $\lambda_i$ (exact sources) and $s_i$
(support-known sources), with $s_i\ge0$, and the feasible set is bounded since $\sum_is_i\le p$.
(ii) and (iii) If a decomposition has weight vector $\lambda$, then so does $t\lambda$ (with scaled
contributions) for every $t\in[0,1]$, which raises $\mu$ continuously up to $1$. Hence the attainable
$\mu$ form $[\mu_{\min}(p),1]$, and the budget cuts this to $[\mu_{\min}(p),\bar\mu]$.
(iv) Each contribution is a nonnegative multiple $\lambda_ia$ of an admissible version $a$ of its
source, so $\langle y,\sum_is_i\rangle\ge\sum_i\lambda_i=1-\mu$, while $\langle y,\sum_is_i\rangle
\le\langle y,p\rangle$. Optimality of the best bound is linear programming duality \cite{Schrijver1986}:
$1-\mu_{\min}(p)$ is the value of $\max\{\sum_i\lambda_i\}$ over (a), (b), and its dual is
$\min\{\langle y,p\rangle\}$ under the stated constraints on $y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Mixture proportion estimation]\label{rem:mpe}
With a single exact source $A$, $\mu_{\min}(p)=1-\kappa^*(p\,|\,A)$, where
$\kappa^*(p\,|\,A)=\min_{b\in\supp A}p(b)/A(b)$ is the maximal proportion of $A$ in $p$ studied in
mixture proportion estimation \cite{Blanchard2010,Scott2015}. A positive budget $\bar\mu$ plays the role
of the bound on the error probability in contaminated sampling \cite{HorowitzManski1995}.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Precision]\label{def:precision}
For a nonempty attribution set, the \emph{width} of source $i$ is $w_i=\max\lambda_i-\min\lambda_i$
over $W_{\bar\mu}(p)$, and the width of the unmodeled weight is $w_\mu=\max\mu-\min\mu$. For an outcome
$b$ with $p(b)>0$, the \emph{observation-level attribution} $\pi_i(b)=s_i(b)/p(b)$ is the probability
that an observation of $b$ came from source $i$; its width is defined likewise. The attribution is
\emph{exact} if all widths vanish. All widths are values of linear programs over $W_{\bar\mu}(p)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Monotone precision ladder]\label{thm:ladder}
Enlarging the admissible versions of any source, or raising the budget, can only enlarge the
attribution set; hence all widths are nondecreasing. In particular, writing $\mu^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\min}$
for the minimal unmodeled weight with exact sources, the attribution sets
\[
W^{(1)}=W^{\mathrm{exact}}_{\mu^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\min}}(p)\ \subseteq\
W^{(2)}=W^{\mathrm{support}}_{\mu^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\min}}(p)\ \subseteq\
W^{(3)}=W^{\mathrm{support}}_{\bar\mu}(p)\qquad(\bar\mu\ge\mu^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\min})
\]
are nonempty and nested, and each width satisfies $w^{(1)}\le w^{(2)}\le w^{(3)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every decomposition with exact sources is one with the corresponding supports, since $\supp(\lambda_i
A_i)\subseteq\supp A_i$; and every decomposition within budget $\bar\mu$ is one within any larger
budget. The width of a coordinate over a larger set is at least its width over a smaller set.
$W^{(1)}$ is nonempty by \cref{thm:attset}(ii).
\end{proof}

We call the increments $w^{(1)}$, $w^{(2)}-w^{(1)}$ and $w^{(3)}-w^{(2)}$ the \emph{structural},
\emph{informational} and \emph{unmodeled} imprecision. They are nonnegative by \cref{thm:ladder}; we do
not claim that they combine additively across sources.

\begin{proposition}[Budget slack forces imprecision]\label{prop:slack}
If $\mu_{\min}(p)<\bar\mu$ and $\mu_{\min}(p)<1$, then $w_\mu=\bar\mu-\mu_{\min}(p)>0$, and every source
whose maximal weight is positive has positive width.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first claim is \cref{thm:attset}(iii). Take a decomposition $\lambda$ with $\mu=\mu_{\min}(p)$.
For $t=(1-\bar\mu)/(1-\mu_{\min}(p))<1$, the scaled family $t\lambda$ is again a decomposition, since its
unmodeled weight is $\bar\mu$. If $\lambda_i>0$, the two decompositions give different values of the
$i$-th weight. If $\lambda_i=0$, this value differs from the positive maximal weight. Either way
$w_i>0$.
\end{proof}

So precision and robustness trade off: a budget for unmodeled sources protects against rejecting the
declared model, and every unit of slack in that budget is paid for in width.

\begin{proposition}[When attribution is exact]\label{prop:exact}
With exact sources and budget $\mu_{\min}(p)=0$, the attribution is exact for every $p$ in
$\conv(A_1,\dots,A_k)$ if and only if the sources are affinely independent. For any sources, if the
exclusive region $R_i=\{b:A_i(b)>0,\ A_j(b)=0\ (j\ne i)\}$ has $A_i(R_i)>0$, then
$\lambda_i=p(R_i)/A_i(R_i)$ in every decomposition, and $\pi_i(b)=\lambda_iA_i(b)/p(b)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is \cite[Prop.~5.7]{Awasthi-Tower}.
\end{proof}

\section{Mechanism classes and attribution of change}\label{sec:mechanisms}
%=====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Mechanism class]
A \emph{mechanism class} for an intervention type assigns to each $p\in\D(B)$ a set
$\Reach(p)\subseteq\D(B)$ of output distributions the intervention may produce from $p$. A change
$p\to p'$ is \emph{reachable} if $p'\in\Reach(p)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Three polyhedral classes]\label{ex:classes}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Source expansion} (adding a corpus): $\Reach(p)=\conv(\cA\cup\mathcal{N})$, where $\cA$
  are the old sources and $\mathcal{N}$ the new ones.
\item \emph{Locality claim} for an edit with declared scope $X_0\subseteq X$ and tolerance $\tau$: at
  a probe outside $X_0$, $\Reach(p)=\{q:\TV(q,p)\le\tau\}$.
\item \emph{Unconstrained edit}: $\Reach(p)=\D(B)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[KL-regularized reinforcement learning is an exponential tilt]\label{prop:gibbs}
For a reward $r\colon B\to\mathbb{R}$ and $\beta>0$, the maximizer of
$\mathbb{E}_q[r]-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,p)$ over $q\in\D(B)$ is
\[
T_r(p)(b)=\frac{p(b)\,e^{r(b)/\beta}}{\sum_cp(c)\,e^{r(c)/\beta}} .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $q\ll p$, $\mathbb{E}_q[r]-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)=\beta\log Z-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(q\|T_r(p))$ with
$Z=\sum_cp(c)e^{r(c)/\beta}$, which is maximal exactly at $q=T_r(p)$; and $q\not\ll p$ gives
$-\infty$. This is the standard Gibbs variational principle, used for RLHF in
\cite{Korbak2022,Rafailov2023}.
\end{proof}

Writing $w(b)=e^{(r(b)-r_{\min})/\beta}$, a reward with values in $[r_{\min},r_{\max}]$ gives weights
in $[1,K]$ with $K=e^{(r_{\max}-r_{\min})/\beta}$, and $T_r(p)$ depends only on $w$. The tilt is a
reweighting by a likelihood, the operation called conditioning in \cite[\S4]{Awasthi-Tower}; it is not
conditioning on an observed event. It preserves support exactly, $\supp T_r(p)=\supp p$, but it can
raise a small probability by a factor up to $K$.

\begin{theorem}[The tilt reach set is a polytope]\label{thm:tilt-polytope}
Let $\Reach_K(p)=\{T_r(p):r(B)\subseteq[r_{\min},r_{\max}]\}$ with $K$ as above. Then
\[
\Reach_K(p)=\big\{q\in\D(B):\ \supp q=\supp p,\ \ q_b\,p_c\le K\,q_c\,p_b\ \text{ for all }b,c\in\supp p\big\},
\]
a convex polytope. Equivalently $q\in\Reach_K(p)$ iff $\supp q=\supp p$ and
\[
K^*(p,q)=\frac{\max_{b\in\supp p}q_b/p_b}{\min_{b\in\supp p}q_b/p_b}\le K,
\]
so $\beta\log K^*(p,q)$ is the least reward range that explains the change.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $q=T_r(p)$, then $q_b/p_b=w_b/Z$ for $b\in\supp p$, so the ratios lie within a factor $K$ of each
other, which is the stated inequality. Conversely, if $q$ satisfies the inequalities and has support
$\supp p$, put $w_b=(q_b/p_b)/\min_c(q_c/p_c)\in[1,K]$ for $b\in\supp p$ and $w_b=1$ elsewhere; then
$T_w(p)=q$. The inequalities are linear in $q$ for fixed $p$; together with $\sum_bq_b=1$ and $q_b=0$
off $\supp p$ they define a polytope. A point satisfying them cannot vanish anywhere on $\supp p$:
if $q_b=0$ then $q_c\,p_b\le K\,q_b\,p_c=0$ for all $c$, contradicting $\sum q=1$. So the constraint
$\supp q=\supp p$ is implied, and the set is closed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Density ratio classes]\label{rem:drc}
The set in \cref{thm:tilt-polytope} is the interval of measures $\{q: p\le c\,q\le Kp\text{ for some
}c>0\}$ of DeRobertis and Hartigan \cite{DeRobertisHartigan1981}, known in robust Bayesian analysis as a
density ratio class. \Cref{thm:tilt-polytope} therefore identifies the reach of KL-regularized RLHF
with bounded rewards as a classical object; what it adds is that identification, and the reading of
$\beta\log K^*$ as the least reward range that explains an observed change.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed reward, unknown temperature]\label{prop:curve}
For a fixed reward $r$, the set $\{T_{r/\beta'}(p):\beta'>0\}$ is a curve, and in general not convex:
for $p$ uniform on three outcomes and $r=(0,1,2)$, the midpoint of the points with $1/\beta'=1$ and
$1/\beta'=3$ is not on the curve.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the curve, $\log(q_b/p_b)-\log(q_0/p_0)$ is proportional to $r_b-r_0$, so $\log(q_2/q_0)=
2\log(q_1/q_0)$ for this $p$ and $r$; the midpoint violates this identity, as checked in
\cref{sec:code}.
\end{proof}

So the geometry of a reach set depends on what is assumed about the mechanism: a bounded reward range
gives a polytope, a fixed reward gives a curve, and no assumption gives everything.

A reachable change can still leave the mechanism's parameters undetermined. For tilts this is
measured exactly.

\begin{proposition}[Identifiability of tilt parameters]\label{prop:reward-ident}
Fix $\beta>0$ and a reward range $[r_{\min},r_{\max}]$ of length $\Delta_r$, and let $q\in\Reach_K(p)$
with $K=e^{\Delta_r/\beta}$. Put $\ell_b=\beta\log(q_b/p_b)$ for $b\in\supp p$. The rewards $r$ with values
in $[r_{\min},r_{\max}]$ and $T_r(p)=q$ are exactly
\[
r_b=\ell_b+c\ \ (b\in\supp p),\qquad c\in\big[r_{\min}-\min_b\ell_b,\ r_{\max}-\max_b\ell_b\big],
\]
with $r_b\in[r_{\min},r_{\max}]$ arbitrary for $b\notin\supp p$.
Hence on $\supp p$ every reward value has width $\Delta_r-\beta\log K^*(p,q)$, reward differences are
identified exactly, and the reward is identified exactly on $\supp p$ if and only if
$\beta\log K^*(p,q)=\Delta_r$. Only $r/\beta$ enters $T_r$, so without a known $\beta$ only the ratios
of reward differences are identified.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$T_r(p)=q$ holds iff $q_b/p_b=e^{r_b/\beta}/Z$ on $\supp p$, that is, iff $r_b-\beta\log(q_b/p_b)$ is
constant on $\supp p$; outcomes outside the support do not affect $T_r(p)$. The range constraint on
$\supp p$ is $r_{\min}\le\ell_b+c\le r_{\max}$ for all $b$, which is the stated interval for $c$; its
length is $\Delta_r-(\max_b\ell_b-\min_b\ell_b)=\Delta_r-\beta\log K^*(p,q)$.
\end{proof}

This mirrors \cref{prop:slack}: slack in the assumed reward range costs precision about the reward,
exactly as slack in the unmodeled budget costs precision about the sources.

\begin{definition}[Residual of a change]
Let $\cone\Reach(p)=\{tq:t\ge0,\ q\in\Reach(p)\}$. An \emph{explained part} of a change $p\to p'$ is a
measure $s\in\cone\Reach(p)$ with $s\le p'$; the remainder $p'-s$ is the \emph{residual}. The
\emph{residual weight} of the change is
\[
\mu^T_{\min}(p,p')=1-\max\{\mass(s):\ s\in\cone\Reach(p),\ s\le p'\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Change decomposition]\label{thm:change}
Let $\Reach(p)$ be closed and convex.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The maximum defining $\mu^T_{\min}(p,p')$ is attained, and $\mu^T_{\min}(p,p')=0$ if and only if
  $p'\in\Reach(p)$.
\item (Certificate) If $y\ge0$ satisfies $\langle y,q\rangle\ge1$ for all $q\in\Reach(p)$, then
  $\mu^T_{\min}(p,p')\ge1-\langle y,p'\rangle$. If $\cone\Reach(p)=\{s\ge0:Gs\le0\}$ is polyhedral, the
  best such bound is attained by a pair $y\ge0$, $\nu\ge0$ with $y+G^{\top}\nu\ge\mathbf{1}$, and it equals
  $\mu^T_{\min}(p,p')$.
\item (Precision of change attribution) The optimal explained parts form a polytope when the cone is
  polyhedral, and the range of each $s(b)$ over it is given by linear programs.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) $\Reach(p)\subseteq\D(B)$ is compact and avoids $0$, so its cone is closed; intersected with
$\{0\le s\le p'\}$ it is compact, and $\mass$ is continuous. If the maximum is $1$, then $s\le p'$ and
$\mass(s)=\mass(p')$ force $s=p'$, so $p'\in\cone\Reach(p)$ with mass one, that is, $p'\in\Reach(p)$.
(ii) For $s=tq$ with $q\in\Reach(p)$, $\mass(s)=t\le t\langle y,q\rangle=\langle y,s\rangle\le\langle
y,p'\rangle$. In the polyhedral case, $\max\{\mathbf 1^{\top}s: Gs\le0,\ s\le p',\ s\ge0\}$ has dual
$\min\{\langle y,p'\rangle:y+G^{\top}\nu\ge\mathbf 1,\ y,\nu\ge0\}$, and strong duality holds
\cite{Schrijver1986}; every dual-feasible pair satisfies $\langle y,s\rangle\ge\mathbf 1^{\top}s-\langle
\nu,Gs\rangle\ge\mathbf 1^{\top}s$ on the cone. (iii) The optimal set is a face of a polytope.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What residuals mean]
A positive residual weight says that at least this much of the observed change is not produced by any
intervention of the declared type. It does not say what produced it. For the tilt class the cone is
$\{s\ge0:\ s_b\,p_c\le K\,s_c\,p_b,\ s=0\text{ off }\supp p\}$, so attributing a change to RLHF is a
linear program with an exact certificate. For an unconstrained edit the residual is always zero: an
edit can be tested only through a claim about it, such as locality.
\end{remark}

Mechanism classes are defined on observed outputs, while real interventions act on model states. The
link between the two is the following.

\begin{definition}[Observable reach and soundness]\label{def:sound}
For an intervention $T\colon\cW\to\cW$ and an observable state $p\in\cP$, the \emph{observable reach}
is
\[
R^\Phi_T(p)=\{\Phi TM:\ M\in\cW,\ \Phi M=p\}.
\]
A mechanism class is \emph{sound} for $T$ if $R^\Phi_T(p)$ is contained, probe by probe, in
$\Reach_T(p)$ for every $p$ in the image of $\Phi$.
\end{definition}

The observable reach can have more than one point even for a deterministic $T$: in the example of
\cref{prop:nonfaithful} it has two. So mechanism classes must be set-valued even for deterministic
interventions.

\begin{proposition}[What a residual certifies]\label{prop:sound}
Let the mechanism class be sound for $T$, and suppose the change $p\to p'$ has positive residual weight
at some probe. Then there is no model $M$ with $\Phi M=p$ and $\Phi TM=p'$: the change was not produced
by $T$ from any model with the observed outputs. Without soundness, the residual certifies only that
the change lies outside the declared class. A zero residual never certifies that $T$ produced the
change.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If such an $M$ existed, then $p'\in R^\Phi_T(p)\subseteq\Reach_T(p)$ at every probe, so the residual
would vanish by \cref{thm:change}(i). The last sentence holds because other interventions may reach the
same outputs.
\end{proof}

The tilt class of \cref{thm:tilt-polytope} is sound for an exact optimizer of the KL-regularized
objective with rewards in the stated range, by \cref{prop:gibbs}. Actual RLHF procedures stop short of
the optimum, so soundness holds only approximately. The following enlargement restores it while
keeping linear programming.

\begin{corollary}[Enlarged classes]\label{cor:enlarge}
If a procedure is known to end within total variation $\varepsilon$ of the exact tilt, then the
enlarged class $\Reach^\varepsilon_K(p)=\{q\in\D(B):\TV(q,q')\le\varepsilon\text{ for some }q'\in
\Reach_K(p)\}$ is sound for it, and it is again a polytope, so \cref{thm:change} applies.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Soundness is immediate from the assumption. The set is the projection onto $q$ of the polyhedron
$\{(q,q',u):q'\in\Reach_K(p),\ -u\le q-q'\le u,\ \sum_bu_b\le2\varepsilon,\ q\in\D(B)\}$, and a bounded
projection of a polyhedron is a polytope.
\end{proof}

How large $\varepsilon$ is for a given procedure is an empirical question; the corollary only says how to
use it once it is known.

\section{Comparing interventions: composition and path defects}\label{sec:composition}
%=====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Quantitative comparison]\label{def:comparison}
For interventions $F,G$ and a set $\cS\subseteq\cW$ of model states,
\[
d_\cS(F,G)=\sup_{M\in\cS}d(\Phi FM,\Phi GM).
\]
This is a pseudometric on interventions. We write $F\approx_\varepsilon G$ on $\cS$ if
$d_\cS(F,G)\le\varepsilon$, and call this a \emph{quantitative comparison}.
\end{definition}

A quantitative comparison records how far two interventions are apart; it is not by itself a 2-cell
in a 2-category, and we do not call it one. \Cref{cor:enriched} identifies the structure in which it is
the hom-metric of an enriched category.

\begin{definition}[Lipschitz on observables]
An intervention $G$ is \emph{$L$-Lipschitz on observables} over $\mathcal{T}\subseteq\cW$ if
$d(\Phi GM,\Phi GM')\le L\,d(\Phi M,\Phi M')$ for all $M,M'\in\mathcal T$. An \emph{output-level
mechanism} is a map $T\colon\cP\to\cP$ acting on observable states; for it the Lipschitz constant is the
usual one for $d$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Lipschitz constants of output-level mechanisms]\label{prop:lipschitz}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Mixing with a fixed target, $p\mapsto(1-a)p+a\,t$, is exactly $(1-a)$-Lipschitz.
\item A tilt with weights in $[1,K]$ is $\tfrac{3K-1}{2}$-Lipschitz, and no constant below $K$ works in
  general.
\item Coarsening along any map of outcomes is $1$-Lipschitz.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) The difference of images is $(1-a)(p-q)$. (iii) Data processing for total variation. (ii) Let
$Z=\langle w,p\rangle\ge1$ and $Z'=\langle w,q\rangle$. Then
$T(p)-T(q)=w\odot(p-q)/Z+w\odot q\,(1/Z-1/Z')$, so
$\lVert T(p)-T(q)\rVert_1\le\big(K\lVert p-q\rVert_1+\lvert Z-Z'\rvert\big)/Z\le K\lVert
p-q\rVert_1+\lvert Z-Z'\rvert$, using $\lVert w\odot q\rVert_1=Z'$ and $Z\ge1$. Since $p-q$ has total mass zero,
$\lvert Z-Z'\rvert=\lvert\langle w-\tfrac{K+1}2\mathbf 1,p-q\rangle\rvert\le\tfrac{K-1}2\lVert
p-q\rVert_1$. For the lower bound take $p=(\epsilon,1-\epsilon)$ and $w=(K,1)$: the first coordinate
of $T(p)$ is $K\epsilon/(1+(K-1)\epsilon)$, whose derivative at $\epsilon=0$ is $K$.
\end{proof}

So tilts can amplify differences, which matters for composition.

\begin{proposition}[No observable Lipschitz constant]\label{prop:nonfaithful}
If $\Phi M=\Phi M'$ but $\Phi GM\ne\Phi GM'$, then $G$ is not $L$-Lipschitz on observables over
$\{M,M'\}$ for any $L$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The left side of the Lipschitz inequality is positive and the right side is zero.
\end{proof}

A weight edit that depends on parts of the model not visible on the probes is of this kind. For such
interventions the composition bound below must use a metric on $\cW$, and conclusions at the probe
level are conditional on that metric.

\begin{theorem}[Composition of quantitative comparisons]\label{thm:composition}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item (Vertical) $d_\cS(F,H)\le d_\cS(F,G)+d_\cS(G,H)$.
\item (Horizontal) If $G'$ is $L$-Lipschitz on observables over $F(\cS)\cup F'(\cS)$, then
  \[
  d_\cS(GF,G'F')\le d_{F(\cS)}(G,G')+L\,d_\cS(F,F').
  \]
\item (Accumulation) For paths $F_n\cdots F_1$ and $G_n\cdots G_1$, let $\cS_0=\cS$ and
  $\cS_j=F_j(\cS_{j-1})$, put $\varepsilon_j=d_{\cS_{j-1}}(F_j,G_j)$, and suppose each $G_j$ is
  $L_j$-Lipschitz on observables over the states reached by both paths. Then
  \[
  \delta_j=d_\cS(F_j\cdots F_1,\,G_j\cdots G_1)\quad\text{satisfies}\quad
  \delta_j\le\varepsilon_j+L_j\delta_{j-1},
  \]
  and hence
  \[
  \delta_n\le\sum_{j=1}^n\varepsilon_j\prod_{i=j+1}^nL_i .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) is the triangle inequality for $d$. (ii) For $M\in\cS$,
\[
d(\Phi GFM,\Phi G'F'M)\le d(\Phi GFM,\Phi G'FM)+d(\Phi G'FM,\Phi G'F'M);
\]
the first term is at most $d_{F(\cS)}(G,G')$ and the second at most $L\,d(\Phi FM,\Phi F'M)$. (iii) Apply (ii) with $F=F_j$, $G=G_j$ to the states $F_{j-1}\cdots F_1M$
and $G_{j-1}\cdots G_1M$, and unroll the recursion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Composition rules of this kind are standard for behavioral pseudometrics of probabilistic systems
\cite{Desharnais2004}, in quantitative algebraic reasoning \cite{MPP2016} and in sensitivity type
systems \cite{ReedPierce2010}. \Cref{thm:composition} is an instance; what is specific here is the
observation map $\Phi$, which is not faithful, and the constants for tilts.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Enriched structure]\label{cor:enriched}
Output-level mechanisms on $\cP$ with Lipschitz constant at most $1$, with $d=d_{\cP}$, form a
category enriched in pseudometric spaces in Lawvere's sense \cite{Lawvere1973}:
$d(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})=0$ and $d(GF,G'F')\le d(G,G')+d(F,F')$. Mixings and coarsenings belong to it; tilts do not, since their
Lipschitz constant can exceed $1$, so in general the structure is the Lipschitz-weighted one of
\cref{thm:composition}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Order and loop defects]
For interventions $E$ and $R$, the \emph{order defect} on $\cS$ is $d_\cS(RE,ER)$. For a sequence
of interventions $T_1,\dots,T_n$, the \emph{loop defect} is $d_\cS(T_n\cdots T_1,\,\mathrm{id})$.
\end{definition}

A positive order defect certifies that the two orders produce different observable models on the
probes; it becomes a causal statement once both orders have actually been run. We call the loop defect
a defect rather than a holonomy, since no connection has been defined.

\begin{proposition}[Coarse evaluation]\label{prop:coarse}
For any coarsening $g$ of outcomes, $\TV(g_*p,g_*q)\le\TV(p,q)$. Hence every order, loop or comparison
defect measured on a coarse evaluation is a lower bound for the fine one, and a positive coarse defect
certifies a positive fine defect.
\end{proposition}

\section{The glue: 1-cells, 2-cells and assembly}\label{sec:glue}
%=====================================================================

The previous sections work probe by probe and compare interventions by numbers. This section
supplies the structure that connects local states: \emph{1-cells} that transport states and their
attribution data, \emph{2-cells} that witness how far two 1-cells differ, and \emph{assembly}, which
asks whether local states over several contexts come from one global state. We state exactly which
laws hold strictly, which hold only as inequalities, and which fail. Every claim below is checked in
\texttt{check\_glue.py} (\cref{sec:code}).

\subsection{Kernel 1-cells}\label{sec:onecells}

\begin{definition}[Kernel 1-cells]\label{def:onecell}
Objects are finite outcome sets. A \emph{1-cell} $k\colon B\rightsquigarrow B'$ is a Markov kernel
$k\colon B\to\D(B')$. It acts on states by Kleisli extension,
$k^\#(p)(b')=\sum_bp(b)\,k(b)(b')$, and on second-order states by $\D(k^\#)$. 1-cells compose by
$(h\circ k)(b)=h^\#(k(b))$, with identities the point masses $\eta_B$.
\end{definition}

Coarsenings, restrictions of a joint outcome to some of its coordinates, noise channels and
mixings $p\mapsto(1-a)p+a\,t$ (the kernel $b\mapsto(1-a)\delta_b+a\,t$) are 1-cells. Tilts are not:
$T_w$ is not affine in $p$, because of the normalization. They are Bayesian updates by the predicate
$w/K$ in the sense of \cite{JacobsZanasi2016}, and they live in the Lipschitz layer of
\cref{sec:composition}, not in the Kleisli category.

\begin{proposition}[Properties of 1-cells]\label{prop:onecell}
For 1-cells $k\colon B\rightsquigarrow B'$ and $h\colon B'\rightsquigarrow B''$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $k^\#$ is affine;
\item $(h\circ k)^\#=h^\#k^\#$ and $\eta_B^\#=\mathrm{id}$;
\item $k^\#\circ\mu=\mu\circ\D(k^\#)$ on second-order states, where $\mu$ is flattening;
\item $\TV(k^\#p,k^\#q)\le\TV(p,q)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i)--(iii) are the Kleisli-category laws of $\D$, and (iii) says that $k^\#$ is a morphism of
free $\D$-algebras. (iv) is the data-processing inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Attribution is transported along 1-cells]\label{prop:transport}
Let $k\colon B\rightsquigarrow B'$. Transport each exact source $A_i$ to $k^\#A_i$ and each support
$S_i$ to $k(S_i)=\bigcup_{b\in S_i}\supp k(b)$. A decomposition $(s_i)$ of $p$ with weights $\lambda$
maps to the decomposition $(k_*s_i)$ of $k^\#p$ with the same weights. Hence
\[
\mu_{\min}(k^\#p)\le\mu_{\min}(p),
\]
and for every budget, each weight interval at $k^\#p$ contains the corresponding interval at $p$:
precision can only be lost along a 1-cell.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pushforward $k_*$ is linear and monotone on nonnegative measures and preserves mass, so
$\sum_ik_*s_i\le k^\#p$, $k_*(\lambda_iA_i)=\lambda_ik^\#A_i$ and $\supp k_*s_i\subseteq k(S_i)$.
Images of decompositions are decompositions with the same weights, which gives both claims.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Attribution as a fibre]
With exact sources and no unmodeled part, the sources form a 1-cell $a\colon I\rightsquigarrow B$,
$i\mapsto A_i$, from the index set of sources, and the attribution set is the fibre
$(a^\#)^{-1}(p)\subseteq\D(I)$. Precision is the size of this fibre, and \cref{prop:transport} is the
inclusion $(a^\#)^{-1}(p)\subseteq((k\circ a)^\#)^{-1}(k^\#p)$, which follows from
$(k\circ a)^\#=k^\#a^\#$. In the demo, coarsening the six outcomes of $x_1$ into three classes lowers
$\mu_{\min}$ at stage 3 from $0.20$ to $0.029$ and widens the weight intervals at budget $0.25$ from
$(0.05,0.125,0.10)$ to $(0.31,0.62,0.31)$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Coupling 2-cells and their metric shadow}\label{sec:twocells}

\begin{definition}[Coupling 2-cells]\label{def:twocell}
Let $f,g\colon X\rightsquigarrow Y$ be 1-cells. A \emph{coupling 2-cell} $\alpha\colon f\Rightarrow g$
is a kernel $\alpha\colon X\to\D(Y\times Y)$ such that $\alpha(x)$ has first marginal $f(x)$ and second
marginal $g(x)$ for every $x$. Its \emph{cost} is
$c(\alpha)=\max_x\Pr_{\alpha(x)}(y\ne y')$. The identity on $f$ is the diagonal coupling
$\Delta_f(x)=\sum_yf(x)(y)\,\delta_{(y,y)}$. The \emph{vertical composite} of $\alpha\colon f\Rightarrow g$
and $\beta\colon g\Rightarrow h$ glues along the common marginal and then forgets it:
\[
(\beta\cdot\alpha)(x)(y,y'')=\sum_{y'\colon g(x)(y')>0}\frac{\alpha(x)(y,y')\,\beta(x)(y',y'')}{g(x)(y')}.
\]
\end{definition}

A coupling is a witness: it pairs the outputs of $f$ and $g$ and shows on which outputs they
disagree. The gluing formula is the gluing lemma for couplings \cite[Ch.~1]{Villani2009}.

\begin{theorem}[The witness layer]\label{thm:witness}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For fixed $X,Y$, 1-cells $X\rightsquigarrow Y$ and coupling 2-cells form a category: vertical
  composition has the right marginals, is associative, and the diagonal couplings are identities.
\item Cost is subadditive, $c(\beta\cdot\alpha)\le c(\alpha)+c(\beta)$, and $c(\Delta_f)=0$.
\item $\min_\alpha c(\alpha)=d(f,g):=\max_x\TV(f(x),g(x))$, attained by the maximal coupling at each
  $x$ \cite{Lindvall1992}.
\item Whiskering by a deterministic map $u\colon W\to X$, $\alpha\mapsto\alpha\circ u$, is strictly
  functorial.
\item Whiskering by a stochastic 1-cell $u\colon W\rightsquigarrow X$ (mixing the couplings) and
  whiskering by a 1-cell $h\colon Y\rightsquigarrow Z$ (copying $h$ on equal pairs and running it
  independently on unequal pairs) preserve identities and marginals and do not increase cost, but
  neither is functorial for vertical composition, even when $h$ is deterministic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Fix $x$ and drop it from the notation. For $\gamma\colon h\Rightarrow k$, both bracketings of the
triple composite equal
$\sum_{y',y''}\alpha(y,y')\beta(y',y'')\gamma(y'',y''')/(g(y')h(y''))$, the sum running over the
supports of $g$ and $h$. Marginals: summing over $y''$
gives $\sum_{y'}\alpha(y,y')=f(x)(y)$, and symmetrically. For the identity,
$(\alpha\cdot\Delta_f)(y,y'')=f(y)\,\alpha(y,y'')/f(y)$. (ii) In the glued triple, $y\ne y''$
implies $y\ne y'$ or $y'\ne y''$. (iii) This is the coupling characterization of total variation.
(iv) Both sides evaluate the same couplings at $u(w)$. (v) Take $X=\{x_0,x_1\}$, $Y=\{0,1\}$,
all three 1-cells constant and uniform, and $\alpha=\beta$ equal to the diagonal at $x_0$ and to the swap coupling at $x_1$.
Then $\beta\cdot\alpha$ is the diagonal at both points, and mixing it with weights
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ gives cost $0$, whereas mixing first gives the independent coupling at each
step, whose composite has cost $\tfrac12$. For postcomposition, let $h$ forget its input,
$h(y)=\nu$ uniform: since $\mathrm{swap}\cdot\mathrm{swap}=\Delta$, the whiskered composite has cost $0$,
while the composite of the whiskered swaps (each the independent coupling of $\nu$ with itself) has
cost $\tfrac12$. A deterministic $h$ merging two of three outcomes also fails; the script exhibits an
instance. The remaining claims are direct.
\end{proof}

So the witness layer has genuine hom-categories, whose composition is gluing, but the natural
whiskerings are not functorial, and therefore it is not a 2-category or a bicategory with these
operations. What survives is the inequality between costs, and that is exactly what the next result
packages strictly.

\begin{definition}[Graded 2-cells]\label{def:graded}
For 1-cells $f,g\colon X\rightsquigarrow Y$, a \emph{graded 2-cell} $f\Rightarrow_\varepsilon g$ is a
number $\varepsilon\ge d(f,g)$. Vertical composition adds grades, the identity on $f$ has grade $0$,
and the horizontal composite of $f\Rightarrow_\varepsilon g$ (with $f,g\colon X\rightsquigarrow Y$) and
$h\Rightarrow_\delta h'$ (with $h,h'\colon Y\rightsquigarrow Z$) is $hf\Rightarrow_{\delta+\varepsilon}h'g$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The graded shadow is a strict 2-category]\label{thm:shadow}
Finite sets, kernel 1-cells and graded 2-cells form a strict 2-category. Every coupling 2-cell
$\alpha\colon f\Rightarrow g$ defines the graded 2-cell $f\Rightarrow_{c(\alpha)}g$; identities go to
identities, and vertical composites go to grades no larger than the sum. The hom-pseudometric $d$
makes kernel 1-cells a category enriched in Lawvere metric spaces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Validity of the composites: vertical by the triangle inequality, horizontal by
\[
d(hf,h'g)\le d(hf,h'f)+d(h'f,h'g)\le d(h,h')+d(f,g),
\]
using convexity of total variation for the first term and \cref{prop:onecell}(iv) for the second.
Since grades compose by addition in a commutative monoid, associativity, unit laws, functoriality of
horizontal composition and the interchange law
$(\delta'+\delta)+(\varepsilon'+\varepsilon)=(\delta'+\varepsilon')+(\delta+\varepsilon)$ all hold.
The statements about couplings are \cref{thm:witness}(ii),(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why tilts break the strict shadow]
For an $L$-Lipschitz map $h'$ the horizontal grade becomes $\delta+L_{h'}\varepsilon$
(\cref{thm:composition}(ii)). Interchange then compares $L_{h''}(\varepsilon+\varepsilon')$ with
$L_{h''}\varepsilon'+L_{h'}\varepsilon$, which agree only if $L_{h'}=L_{h''}$. This is the 2-categorical
reading of \cref{cor:enriched}: the strict structure holds for non-expansive maps, and tilts enter
with Lipschitz weights.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Assembly: gluing local states}\label{sec:assembly}

\begin{definition}[Contexts and global sections \cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}]\label{def:contexts}
Let $V$ be a finite set of observables with outcome sets $O_v$, and let $\mathcal C$ be a cover of $V$
by \emph{contexts}. For $C'\subseteq C$ the restriction $r^C_{C'}\colon O^C\to O^{C'}$ is a
deterministic 1-cell. An \emph{empirical model} is a family $e=(e_C\in\D(O^C))_{C\in\mathcal C}$; it is
\emph{compatible} if $e_C$ and $e_{C'}$ restrict to the same distribution on $C\cap C'$. A
\emph{global section} is $g\in\D(O^V)$ with $(r^V_C)^\#g=e_C$ for every $C$.
\end{definition}

In provenance terms, an observable is a probe, and a context is a set of probes whose outputs are
recorded jointly, for instance several questions answered in one conversation, or one evaluation
item scored on several criteria. Per-probe evaluation is the case of singleton contexts, in which
every family glues. We call gluing along restriction 1-cells \emph{1-cell gluing}; it is an exact
condition. \emph{2-cell gluing} is gluing of witnesses, and assembly up to a graded 2-cell.

\begin{proposition}[Two contexts: 1-cell gluing is 2-cell composition]\label{prop:twoctx}
Let $C_1,C_2$ be contexts with $I=C_1\cap C_2$, and let $e_1,e_2$ be compatible with common
restriction $e_I$. Then
\[
g(a,i,c)=\frac{e_1(a,i)\,e_2(i,c)}{e_I(i)}\qquad(a\in O^{C_1\setminus I},\ i\in O^I,\ c\in O^{C_2\setminus I})
\]
(read as $0$ when $e_I(i)=0$) is a global section. Regarding $e_1$ as a coupling of its $C_1\setminus I$ marginal with $e_I$, and
$e_2$ as a coupling of $e_I$ with its $C_2\setminus I$ marginal, the marginal of $g$ that forgets $I$
is the vertical composite $e_2\cdot e_1$ of \cref{def:twocell}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Summing $g$ over $c$ gives $e_1$, and over $a$ gives $e_2$. Forgetting $i$ gives the gluing formula of
\cref{def:twocell}.
\end{proof}

For more than two contexts, compatibility is not enough: a classical theorem of Vorob'ev states that
every compatible family over a cover extends to a global section precisely when the cover is
acyclic, in the sense that its contexts can be ordered so that each one meets the union of its
predecessors inside a single predecessor \cite{Vorobev1962}. The four contexts of the
Clauser--Horne--Shimony--Holt (CHSH) scenario form a cycle, and the Popescu--Rohrlich (PR) box is
compatible on every overlap without having a global section (\cref{sec:code}). The next theorem measures
the failure with the machinery of \cref{sec:attribution}.

\begin{theorem}[Contextual fraction is minimal unmodeled weight]\label{thm:cf}
Let $e$ be a compatible empirical model with $m=\lvert\mathcal C\rvert$ contexts. Put
$p_e=\tfrac1m(e_C)_{C\in\mathcal C}$, a distribution on the disjoint union $\bigsqcup_CO^C$, and, for each
deterministic global assignment $s\in O^V$, declare the exact source
$A_s=\tfrac1m(\delta_{s|_C})_{C\in\mathcal C}$. Then
\[
\mu_{\min}(p_e)=\mathrm{CF}(e),
\]
the contextual fraction of Abramsky, Barbosa and Mansfield \cite{ABM2017}. In particular $e$ has a
global section if and only if $\mu_{\min}(p_e)=0$, and a certificate $y$ of \cref{thm:attset}(iv) is
an inequality $\langle y,A_s\rangle\ge1$ satisfied by every global assignment and violated by $e$
whenever $\langle y,p_e\rangle<1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Mixtures $\sum_s\lambda_sA_s$ with $\sum_s\lambda_s=1$ are exactly the vectors $p_g$ of the restriction
families of global distributions $g=\sum_s\lambda_s\delta_s$. In a decomposition
$p_e=\sum_s\lambda_sA_s+\mu U$, every context block of $\sum_s\lambda_sA_s$ has mass
$(1-\mu)/m$, so every block of $\mu U$ has mass $\mu/m$, and $m$ times each block of $U$ is a distribution $e'_C$; the family $e'$ is
compatible because $e$ and the restriction family of $g$ are. Thus decompositions are exactly the
expressions $e=(1-\mu)e^{\mathrm{NC}}+\mu e'$ with $e^{\mathrm{NC}}$ noncontextual, and minimizing $\mu$
is the linear program defining $\mathrm{CF}$ in \cite{ABM2017}.
\end{proof}

For the PR box mixed with uniform noise at visibility $v$, the linear program gives
$\mathrm{CF}=\max(0,2v-1)$ at $v\in\{1,\tfrac34,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\}$, with exactly verified
certificates. The same construction with \emph{declared} sources in place of deterministic
assignments asks a provenance question.

\begin{example}[Prompt-independent attribution]\label{ex:global-attr}
At stage 2 of \cref{sec:demo}, the corpus weights are identified exactly at each probe, with
$\mu_{\min}=0$ probe by probe, but they differ across probes. Declaring the sources $A_1,A_2,N$
on all probes jointly and asking for one weight vector, as in \cref{thm:cf}, gives
$\mu_{\min}=\tfrac1{10}$ on $\{x_1,x_2\}$ and $\tfrac3{10}$ on $\{x_1,x_2,x_3\}$, both certified exactly.
So no prompt-independent mixture of the declared corpora explains the joint behaviour, and $\tfrac3{10}$
is the least mass that any such explanation leaves unexplained. The local answers glue only if the
explanation is allowed to depend on the prompt.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Approximate gluing]\label{def:epsstar}
For any empirical model $e$, compatible or not,
\[
\varepsilon^*(e)=\min_{g\in\D(O^V)}\ \max_{C\in\mathcal C}\TV\big((r^V_C)^\#g,\ e_C\big).
\]
A minimizer is a global state that agrees with every context up to the graded 2-cell of grade
$\varepsilon^*$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Bounds for approximate gluing]\label{prop:epsstar}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\varepsilon^*(e)$ is the value of a linear program, and it is attained.
\item $\varepsilon^*(e)\ge\tfrac12\max_{C,C'}\TV\big(e_C|_{C\cap C'},\,e_{C'}|_{C\cap C'}\big)$.
\item If $e$ is compatible, $\varepsilon^*(e)\le\mathrm{CF}(e)$, and $\varepsilon^*(e)=0$ if and only if
  $e$ has a global section.
\item For the PR box, $\varepsilon^*=\tfrac14$, whereas $\mathrm{CF}=1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) Introduce slack variables for the absolute values. (ii) For any $g$, by the triangle inequality and
data processing,
$\TV(e_C|_I,e_{C'}|_I)\le\TV(e_C,g|_C)+\TV(g|_{C'},e_{C'})\le2\max_C\TV(g|_C,e_C)$.
(iii) Write $e=(1-\kappa)e^{\mathrm{NC}}+\kappa e'$ with $\kappa=\mathrm{CF}(e)$ and let $g$ be a global
section of $e^{\mathrm{NC}}$. Then $\TV(g|_C,e_C)=\kappa\,\TV(e^{\mathrm{NC}}_C,e'_C)\le\kappa$. The
equivalence follows since $\varepsilon^*=0$ means that a global section exists.
(iv) The PR box in context $(i,j)$ is uniform on the outcomes with $o_1\oplus o_2=ij$. Every deterministic
assignment violates this parity in an odd number of the four contexts, since the four left-hand
sides sum to $0$ and the right-hand sides to $1$ modulo $2$. Hence for any $g$ the expected number of
violated contexts is at least $1$, some context is violated with probability at least $\tfrac14$, and
$\TV(g|_C,e_C)$ is at least that probability. The uniform distribution on the eight assignments with
exactly one violation attains $\tfrac14$ in every context.
\end{proof}

For the noisy PR family the linear program gives $\varepsilon^*=\tfrac14\mathrm{CF}$ at $v=\tfrac34$ and
$v=\tfrac9{10}$, so the bound in (iii) can be loose by a factor of four. The two numbers answer
different questions. $\mathrm{CF}$ is the weight that must be attributed to something outside every
global explanation, while $\varepsilon^*$ is how far the best single global state is from the worst
context.

\paragraph{Gluing at level 2.} The tower's second level carries uncertainty about output
distributions, for instance a posterior over a model's predictive distribution. Gluing there is
finer than gluing the flattened states.

\begin{proposition}[Two-context gluing at level 2]\label{prop:level2}
Let $P_1\in\D^2(O^{C_1})$ and $P_2\in\D^2(O^{C_2})$ have finite support and the same image
$Q=\D((r_I)^\#)P_1=\D((r_I)^\#)P_2$ on the overlap $I$. Define
\[
G=\sum_q Q(q)\sum_{p_1,p_2}P_1(p_1\mid q)\,P_2(p_2\mid q)\;\delta_{p_1\otimes_qp_2},
\]
where $p_1\otimes_qp_2$ is the gluing of \cref{prop:twoctx}. Then $G$ restricts to $P_1$ and to $P_2$,
and its flattening $\mu G$ is a global section of the pair $(\mu P_1,\mu P_2)$. In general
$\mu G\ne\mu P_1\otimes\mu P_2$: the level-2 gluing keeps a correlation between the contexts that runs
through the uncertainty about the overlap.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each $p_1\otimes_qp_2$ restricts to $p_1$ and $p_2$ by \cref{prop:twoctx}, and $G$ is the gluing of
$P_1$ and $P_2$ along $Q$, so its restrictions are $P_1$ and $P_2$. For the flattening,
$(r_{C_1})^\#\mu G=\mu\,\D((r_{C_1})^\#)G=\mu P_1$ by \cref{prop:onecell}(iii). The script gives an
instance in which $\TV(\mu G,\mu P_1\otimes\mu P_2)=0.015$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The layers}

\begin{center}\small

\paragraph{Why each layer is needed.} The monad is needed because attribution \emph{is} mixture
decomposition: a decomposition is a preimage under the algebra structure of $\D$. The local comonad
is needed because what counts as an admissible belief depends on context, which is the subject of
\cite{Awasthi-Tower}. 1-cells are needed because every stage of a model's history (coarsening an
evaluation, restricting to a context, mixing in data) must carry attribution data from one state to
the next, and \cref{prop:transport} says this transport never manufactures precision. 2-cells are
needed because ``these two paths differ by $0.0985$'' (\cref{sec:demo}) is a claim about a pair of
1-cells. The shadow composes such claims strictly, and a witness shows \emph{where} the paths
disagree, as a joint distribution of their outputs. Assembly is needed because real evaluations
record several probes jointly, and \cref{ex:global-attr} shows that exact local attributions need not
come from any single global explanation. The geometry is needed for the metric, and it is where a
Fukaya-type structure would enter.

\paragraph{What is categorical here.} A reader may ask why a paper about 2-categorical glue spends
most of its pages on polytopes and linear programs. The answer is that the linear programs
\emph{compute} categorical objects:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item an attribution set is a fibre of the Kleisli extension of a 1-cell (the remark after
  \cref{prop:transport});
\item the minimal unmodeled weight of a multi-context state is the failure of the sheaf condition
  (\cref{thm:cf});
\item a reach set is the image of a family of maps of observable states, which for tilts are
  Bayesian predicate updates \cite{JacobsZanasi2016};
\item comparisons are the hom-metric of a Lawvere-enriched category and the grades of a strict
  2-category (\cref{thm:shadow});
\item witnesses are couplings, and their composition is the gluing lemma (\cref{thm:witness}).
\end{itemize}
Category theory fixes what the objects are and how they compose. Convex geometry and duality are how
one computes them on a finite probe set, with certificates. Neither replaces the other.

\paragraph{What is strict and what is not.} Kernels form a strict category, the witness
hom-categories are strict, and the graded shadow is a strict 2-category. The witness layer with its
natural whiskerings is not a 2-category: functoriality fails, with explicit counterexamples, and only
the cost inequality survives. Tilts are not kernels, and with them the shadow keeps its composition
rule but loses strict interchange. Exact gluing holds for two contexts and for acyclic covers, and
fails on cycles, where $\mathrm{CF}$ and $\varepsilon^*$ measure the failure.

\subsection{Geometry and the Fukaya programme}\label{sec:fukaya}

\begin{proposition}[The simplex as a moment polytope]\label{prop:geometry}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The map $p\mapsto\sqrt p$ pulls the round metric of the unit sphere back to one quarter of the
  Fisher metric: $\sum_bv_b^2/p_b=4\sum_b\big(v_b/(2\sqrt{p_b})\big)^2$. Consequently, the Fisher--Rao
  distance is $2\arccos\sum_b\sqrt{p_bq_b}$ \cite{Wootters1981,AmariNagaoka2000}.
\item The map $[\psi]\mapsto(\lvert\psi_b\rvert^2/\lVert\psi\rVert^2)_b$ is the moment map of the
  standard torus action on $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$ with the Fubini--Study form, and its image is the
  simplex $\D(B)$ with $\lvert B\rvert=n$. The fibre over an interior $p$ is a Lagrangian torus
  $T^{n-1}$, the set of amplitudes with $\lvert\psi_b\rvert^2=p_b$, which differ only in their phases.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) is a direct computation; the distance follows because $\sqrt p$ lies on the unit sphere, where
the geodesic distance is $\arccos\langle\sqrt p,\sqrt q\rangle$. (ii) is standard toric geometry
\cite{Delzant1988}. Isotropy of the fibre is checked in \texttt{check\_glue.py} in an affine chart of
$\mathbb{CP}^2$: the Fubini--Study form vanishes on pairs of phase-rotation vectors.
\end{proof}

So an output distribution is a point of a moment polytope, and the information that observation
discards (the phases of an amplitude realizing it) is a Lagrangian torus. This is the precise
sense in which symplectic geometry sits under the simplex. It is a geometric fact about the ambient
space, and nothing in this paper depends on it.

\paragraph{What the Fukaya category of $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$ sees.} The objects of a Fukaya category are
Lagrangians with brane data, its morphisms are Floer cochains, and its compositions count holomorphic
polygons, so that composition is defined by gluing and is associative only up to coherent higher
homotopies. For the torus fibres of $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$ the answer is sharp. A fibre with a flat
$U(1)$ connection has nonvanishing Floer cohomology exactly when it lies over a critical point of
the disc potential \cite{ChoOh2006,FOOO2010}. For $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$ the only such fibre is the
Clifford torus over the barycentre, whose Floer cohomology Cho computed \cite{Cho2004}. In statistical terms, only
the uniform distribution gives a nonzero object. We know of no statistical meaning for this fact, and
we conclude that the ambient $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$ is not the right symplectic manifold for attribution.

\paragraph{Necessary conditions for a genuine connection.} We state the programme as conditions that
any construction would have to meet. We do not claim that any of them holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(F\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{A manifold built from the data.} Attribution sets and bounded-reward reach sets are
  polytopes (\cref{thm:attset,thm:tilt-polytope}), with rational facet normals when the data are
  rational. A compact polytope is the moment image of a compact toric symplectic manifold if and only if
  it is Delzant \cite{Delzant1988}, and of a toric orbifold if and only if it is simple and rational
  \cite{LermanTolman1997}. When the condition holds, precision widths become lengths in a moment
  polytope and torus fibres sit over individual explanations. Attribution polytopes need not be
  simple, so this already restricts the cases covered.
\item \emph{Higher compositions for witnesses.} \Cref{thm:witness}(v) shows that the witness layer
  composes only up to an inequality. A Fukaya-type structure would need these failures to be organized
  as higher operations satisfying the $A_\infty$ relations. We have not constructed such operations.
\item \emph{Assembly as composition.} \Cref{prop:twoctx} identifies two-context gluing with
  vertical composition of witnesses. A geometric realization should send this identification to
  composition of Lagrangian morphisms, and send the obstruction measured by $\mathrm{CF}$ and
  $\varepsilon^*$ on cyclic covers to an obstruction to composition.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why pursue it.} The two features that distinguish the witness layer from a plain metric,
composition by gluing and laws that hold only up to controlled error, are also the defining features of
Fukaya categories. That is an analogy, not a theorem. Its value is that (F1)--(F3) are checkable: each is
a finite statement about specific polytopes, couplings and covers.

\section{Relation to prior work}\label{sec:related}
%=====================================================================

Most individual ingredients of this paper are known; the contribution is their combination into a
calculus for model provenance whose reported object is a set of explanations together with its width
and a certificate. We state the relation area by area and then summarize.

\paragraph{Mixture proportion estimation and contaminated data.} Mixture proportion estimation studies
$F=(1-\kappa)G+\kappa H$ with $H$ known and $G$ unknown; the maximal proportion
$\kappa^*(F|H)=\operatorname{ess\,inf}f/h$ is identified, and $\kappa$ itself is identified under
irreducibility \cite{Blanchard2010,Scott2015}, with extensions beyond irreducibility \cite{Zhu2023}.
Several known components plus one unknown appear in class-proportion estimation with anomaly rejection
\cite{SandersonScott2014}, in open-set label shift, where identifiability needs positivity or
separability conditions \cite{Garg2022}, and in mutual contamination models \cite{BlanchardScott2014}.
In econometrics, Horowitz and Manski derive sharp bounds under contaminated sampling with a known
bound on the error probability \cite{HorowitzManski1995}, within the program of partial
identification \cite{Manski2003}. Our minimal unmodeled weight is the finite-outcome complement of
$\kappa^*$ (\cref{rem:mpe}), and our budget is the Horowitz--Manski error bound. These works estimate
proportions from samples under identifying assumptions. We instead treat output distributions as
known, characterize the \emph{whole} set of decompositions whether or not it is identified, allow
sources known exactly and sources known only by their supports in the same model, report sharp
intervals for every weight and every observation-level attribution, and attach exact certificates.

\paragraph{Imprecise probability.} Attribution sets are credal sets of decompositions in the sense of
imprecise probability \cite{Walley1991}; for sources known by their supports, the weight intervals are
belief and plausibility of Dempster--Shafer theory \cite{Dempster1967,Shafer1976}
(\cite[Prop.~5.8]{Awasthi-Tower}). The density ratio classes of robust Bayesian analysis
\cite{DeRobertisHartigan1981} are exactly the reach sets of bounded-reward RLHF (\cref{rem:drc}). What
is new here is the precision ladder that separates structural, informational and unmodeled
imprecision (\cref{thm:ladder}), and the fact that slack in the unmodeled budget always costs precision
(\cref{prop:slack}).

\paragraph{RLHF.} The Gibbs form of the optimum of KL-regularized reinforcement learning is known
\cite{Korbak2022,Rafailov2023}; we use it. The new point is a membership test for the resulting class of
changes and a certified residual when a change falls outside it (\cref{thm:tilt-polytope,thm:change}).

\paragraph{Training-data attribution.} Influence functions \cite{KohLiang2017}, datamodels
\cite{Ilyas2022} and TRAK \cite{Park2023} estimate how individual training examples affect predictions,
approximating counterfactual retraining. They answer a different question. We take sources as given,
for instance reference models trained on each corpus, and certify what observed output distributions
do and do not determine about them. The two are complementary: data-attribution estimates can be used
to construct declared sources.

\paragraph{Model editing, diffing and composition.} Locality or specificity of weight edits is measured
as an evaluation metric \cite{Meng2022}, and edits are known to have ripple effects beyond their
intended scope \cite{Cohen2024}. Here a locality claim is a mechanism class, and its violation comes
with a certified residual (\cref{ex:classes}, \cref{thm:change}). Crosscoder-based model diffing compares
the internal features of a base and a fine-tuned model \cite{Minder2025}; our comparisons are
behavioral, at the probe outputs. Task arithmetic composes interventions as vectors in weight space
\cite{Ilharco2023}; the order defect measures whether composition commutes in the outputs.

\paragraph{Metric semantics.} Categories enriched in generalized metric spaces go back to Lawvere
\cite{Lawvere1973}; behavioral pseudometrics for probabilistic systems \cite{Desharnais2004},
quantitative algebraic reasoning \cite{MPP2016} and sensitivity type systems \cite{ReedPierce2010} all
use composition rules with Lipschitz factors. \Cref{thm:composition} is an instance. What is specific
here is that interventions act on model states and are compared only through a non-faithful
observation map, which can rule out any Lipschitz constant (\cref{prop:nonfaithful}), and the explicit
constants for tilts (\cref{prop:lipschitz}).

\paragraph{Contextuality and comonadic simulation.} The relation to contextuality is one of extension,
not replacement. Abramsky and Brandenburger formulate an empirical model as compatible local probability
data and characterize contextuality by the existence of a global section
\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}. Abramsky, Barbosa, Karvonen and Mansfield then gave a comonadic
treatment of simulations, that is, of morphisms between empirical models \cite{Abramsky2019}. Morphisms
are therefore not our novelty. Our local object is richer: for each base $B$ it contains the ambient
probability structure together with a base-dependent context comonad, a mixed distributive law,
higher-order admissibility data and an obstruction tower \cite{Awasthi-Tower}. The maps considered here
are meant to transport these whole local structures, and the present paper equips such transport with
quantitative comparison and path defects. For the global problem, \cref{thm:cf} shows that the
contextual fraction of Abramsky, Barbosa and Mansfield \cite{ABM2017} is the minimal unmodeled weight of
\cref{sec:attribution} for a particular choice of sources, namely the deterministic global assignments.
Declaring reference models as sources instead turns the same linear program into a test for
prompt-independent attribution (\cref{ex:global-attr}). Abramsky and Brandenburger work with sections
and do not use 2-cells. Here two-context gluing is identified with vertical composition of coupling
2-cells (\cref{prop:twoctx}), approximate assembly is graded by the shadow (\cref{prop:epsstar}), and
gluing is carried out at the second level of the tower (\cref{prop:level2}). The extension criterion for
acyclic covers is Vorob'ev's \cite{Vorobev1962}.

\paragraph{Couplings, information geometry and toric geometry.} The gluing lemma and the coupling
characterization of total variation are standard \cite{Villani2009,Lindvall1992}. Our use of them is
to organize witnesses of comparisons into hom-categories and to show where functoriality fails
(\cref{thm:witness}). Bayesian updating as predicate transformation is due to Jacobs and Zanasi
\cite{JacobsZanasi2016}. The square-root embedding and the Fisher--Rao distance are classical
\cite{Wootters1981,AmariNagaoka2000}, and the moment map of $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$, Delzant's theorem and
the Floer theory of toric fibres are standard \cite{Delzant1988,LermanTolman1997,Cho2004,ChoOh2006,FOOO2010}.
Nothing in \cref{sec:fukaya} is a new theorem. What is new there is the list of conditions
(F1)--(F3) that a Fukaya-type realization of attribution would have to satisfy.

\section{Scope and open problems}\label{sec:scope}
%=====================================================================

\paragraph{Local structure and glue.} In the architecture of \cite{Awasthi-Tower}, the probability monad
$\D$ is ambient, and each base $B$ carries local structure: a context comonad, a distributive law,
admissibility data and an obstruction tower. This paper supplies the transport between local
structures: interventions, mechanism classes and quantitative comparison, and, in \cref{sec:glue},
kernel 1-cells, coupling 2-cells and assembly. Deterministic base change is a structure-preserving map
in \cite[Prop.~9.13]{Awasthi-Tower}. Kernel 1-cells act on the first two levels compatibly with
flattening (\cref{prop:onecell}); whether they act on the full local structure, including the context
comonad and the mixed law, is not yet established.

\paragraph{Structural provenance.} The obstruction tower of \cite[\S10]{Awasthi-Tower} gives a second
coordinate. For a claim $c$ attached to a model, let $k(c)$ be its first failing level. Along an
intervention, the pair $(k_{\mathrm{before}},k_{\mathrm{after}})$ records which level of admissibility
changed. Because failure is monotone up the tower \cite[Thm.~10.5]{Awasthi-Tower}, a transition
$k:1\to2$ means that a level-one obstruction was removed while a level-two one remains; it does not
mean that a level-two obstruction was created. We leave the systematic study of such transitions to
future work.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Finite-sample precision.} Output distributions are estimated from samples. Replacing $p$ by
  a confidence region $\mathcal C_\delta$ turns each linear program into a robust program and each width
  into a statistical width.
\item \emph{Weight-level metrics.} Choose a metric on $\cW$ for which real interventions are Lipschitz,
  so that \cref{thm:composition} applies beyond output-level mechanisms.
\item \emph{Higher levels.} Lift comparisons to second- and third-order states with the Kantorovich
  metric \cite{FritzPerrone2019}, and relate them to the obstruction tower.
\item \emph{Cyclic covers at level 2.} \Cref{prop:level2} glues two contexts at level 2. For cyclic
  covers, define and compute a level-2 contextual fraction and compare it with the level-1 value of
  the flattened model.
\item \emph{A functorial witness layer.} Find whiskerings of coupling 2-cells that are functorial, for
  example by indexing couplings by pairs of inputs as in relational program logics, or prove that none
  exist for noisy 1-cells.
\item \emph{Fukaya conditions.} Decide (F1)--(F3) of \cref{sec:fukaya} for the attribution polytopes
  that arise in practice.
\item \emph{Empirical validation.} Run the provenance report on real checkpoints, with declared sources
  from reference models.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Code and computational checks}\label{sec:code}

The library \texttt{provenance.py} implements attribution sets, widths, residuals and certificates.
The script \texttt{check\allowbreak\_attribution.py} verifies the following.
\cref{thm:attset} (nonemptiness criterion, the interval $[\mu_{\min},\bar\mu]$, and exactly verified dual
certificates); \cref{thm:ladder} (monotone widths along the ladder and in the budget);
\cref{prop:slack}; \cref{thm:tilt-polytope} in exact arithmetic (every tilt satisfies the inequalities,
every point satisfying them is reconstructed exactly as a tilt, and mixtures of tilts are tilts);
\cref{prop:curve}; \cref{thm:change} for the tilt class (exactly verified certificates $(y,\nu)$, and
zero residual exactly for reachable changes); \cref{prop:lipschitz} (the exact constant for mixing, the
tilt bound, and an example with amplification $4.0$ for $K=4$); \cref{prop:nonfaithful};
\cref{thm:composition}(i)--(iii) in exact arithmetic; \cref{prop:coarse}; \cref{prop:reward-ident} (the
reward interval reproduces the tilt at both endpoints, leaves the range just beyond them, and has
width $\Delta_r-\beta\log K^*$); the observable reach of the example of \cref{prop:nonfaithful} has two
points, so \cref{prop:sound} needs set-valued classes; and \cref{cor:enlarge} (the residual relative
to the enlarged class vanishes for perturbed tilts within $\varepsilon$ and is positive outside). All 24
checks pass.
The script \texttt{check\allowbreak\_glue.py} verifies \cref{sec:glue,sec:architecture} in 30 checks.
\emph{1-cells:} \cref{prop:onecell}(i)--(iv) in exact arithmetic, and \cref{prop:transport} for a
coarsening and a noisy coarsening of the demo state. \emph{2-cells:} the marginals, associativity and
identities of \cref{thm:witness}(i) exactly, including couplings with zeros; subadditivity of cost; minimal
cost equal to total variation by linear programming; strict functoriality for deterministic
precomposition; the two counterexamples of \cref{thm:witness}(v) and a counterexample for a deterministic
merging map; the laws of \cref{thm:shadow}. \emph{Assembly:} \cref{prop:twoctx}; \cref{thm:cf} for the
noisy PR family, with exactly verified certificates; compatibility of the PR box on all overlaps;
\cref{prop:level2}; \cref{prop:epsstar}(ii)--(iv), with an exact feasible section and the parity lower
bound for $\varepsilon^*=\tfrac14$; \cref{ex:global-attr}. \emph{Geometry:} \cref{prop:geometry}, the
Fisher--Rao distance by integrating along a great circle, and isotropy of the torus fibres in
$\mathbb{CP}^2$. All 30 checks pass.
The script \texttt{provenance\allowbreak\_demo.py} produces the report of \cref{sec:demo}. Linear programs are solved in
floating point, and their certificates are rounded to rationals and verified exactly.
